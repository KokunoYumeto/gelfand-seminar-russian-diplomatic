\noindent This companion indexes the supplied 1988--1989 material as
22 catalogue records and 160 mathematical occurrences. The order follows
the manuscript, including record 760 before 759. The 1989 occurrences
retain the academic-year prefix \texttt{1988-}. Page P001477 continues
record 751, already represented by \texttt{1987-751.8--10}; it is not
duplicated as record 753. No genuine 1990--1991 session is present.

Two dates require reconciliation. P001514 reads \texttt{13/II-88},
whereas the catalogue, chronology and weekday identify 13 February 1989.
P001520 reads \texttt{15/V-89}, placing the Drinfeld--Dotsenko session
on 15 May rather than the catalogue's 14 May. Diplomatic readings remain
in the correction ledger. A faithful notebook error, an uncertain reading
and an editorial mathematical repair are different kinds of evidence.

The strongest publication matches concern projective duality and
hyperdeterminants, Clebsch hydrodynamics and quasi-Hopf algebras.
The first joint Gelfand--Zelevinsky--Kapranov note was received on
13 December 1988 and published in 1989. Its jet complexes, degree series
and boundary formulas closely match the notebook programme. The Segre
hypersurface criterion has the earlier Knop--Menzel antecedent explicitly
acknowledged by the joint paper
\citep[section 6]{gelfandzelevinskykapranov1989dual,knopmenzel1987duale}.
The subsequent Cayley--Koszul paper, received in June 1989, distinguishes
regular and logarithmic constructions and their outputs
\citep{gelfandzelevinskykapranov1989cayley}.

Zakharov's paper, submitted two days after the 31 October session,
allows an unusually close comparison of the current algebra and
incompleteness obstruction
\citep[sections 2--3, 5--6]{zakharov1989clebsch}. Its negative result
concerns a specified family of integrals, not universal nonintegrability
of two-dimensional Euler flow. Drinfeld's paper was received on
23 May 1989, eight days after the corrected seminar date. Its formal
deformation setting and tensor conventions are essential; part of a
classification proof is expressly deferred
\citep[section 1 and Theorem 3.15]{drinfeld1989quasi}.

The classifications total A:33, B:3, C:1, D:0, E:107 and F:16.
They describe historical and evidential relations, not importance.
A pre-existing result belongs to E even when central to the discussion.
F records insufficient specification or identification; it does not mean
that a precise conjecture remains open. Seven problem identities connect
repeated questions without attaching all surrounding background to them.

The recorded request for an explicit BGG proof is distinguished from
earlier existence and explicit-map problems. It is not explicitly
attributed to I.\ M.\ Gelfand. Later constructive treatments substantially
address its operational interpretation, without certifying elimination
of every categorical argument. Similarly, finite-dimensional
coquasi-Hopf duality addresses a qualified duality question; an
infinite-dimensional full dual generally needs restriction or topology.

Only \texttt{1988-774.17} carries the final Ising problem identity.
Its preceding formulas provide prerequisites and interpretive evidence.
The development proves finite graph expansions, restores normalization
and boundary factors, distinguishes local surface closure from periodic
admissibility, derives connected energy covariance and proves lattice
shell-growth estimates. These are established finite facts with proofs
supplied here, not a new construction of the three-dimensional critical
theory. The stronger field-convergence target is explicitly editorial.
Planar spin-correlation theorems \citep{chelkak2015spin}, conditional
bootstrap results and effective descriptions of a gapped phase have
different conclusions from that target.

Every item separates recorded content, modern statement, reconstruction,
literal correctness, publication match and verification level.
Bibliographic \texttt{verified} means the source identity or URL was
checked; annotations distinguish reading a result from checking metadata
or an indirect attribution. Status statements are bounded by the sources
actually examined as of November 2026.
