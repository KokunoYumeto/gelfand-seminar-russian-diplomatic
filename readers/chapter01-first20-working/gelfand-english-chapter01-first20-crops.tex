\subsection*{Pressure sign and boundary term (source frames 4–5)}

For a separate, explicit lattice model, let an interior subcube contain \(h^\nu\) sites, with unit attractive nearest-neighbor bonds. Each of its \(2\nu\) faces has \(h^{\nu-1}\) outgoing bonds. The coupling energy \(\gamma\) is minus the number of occupied crossing bonds, so
\[|\gamma|\le 2\nu h^{\nu-1}.\]
With fixed \(v=V/N\), \(V=L^\nu\), and fixed \(h/L\), this is \(O(N^{(\nu-1)/\nu})\); in dimension three it is \(O(N^{2/3})\). This explains a possible relation between the two powers without changing the tightly written exponent on \(V\).

\subsection*{Walks, linearization and image measures (source frames 11--15)}
These conditional calculations do not correct the diplomatic transcription.

On the square lattice, after a fixed directed first edge, a path that forbids immediate reversal has at most three choices for each next edge. Thus there are at most $3^{k-1}$ such words of length $k$ with that first edge fixed, and $4\cdot3^{k-1}$ if the initial direction is free. Closed boundaries are a subset of those words, subject to their anchoring conventions. This explains a possible factor $3^k$; it does not identify the abbreviated lemma or its exact objects.

For the separate walk that chooses uniformly among the three directions other than immediate reversal, let $D_j$ be its unit increments and $S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nD_j$. Conditional expectation gives $\mathbb E(D_{j+1}\mid D_j)=D_j/3$, hence $\mathbb E(D_i\cdot D_j)=3^{-|i-j|}$. Therefore
\[
\mathbb E|S_n|^2=n+2\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}(n-h)3^{-h}
=2n-\frac32+\frac32\,3^{-n},\qquad
\mathbb E|S_n|\leq\sqrt{2n}.
\]
Exact enumeration through eight steps confirms this identity. The corresponding counts of self-avoiding paths through steps one to eight are $4,12,36,100,284,780,2172,5916$, whereas the nonbacktracking counts are $4\cdot3^{n-1}$. Avoiding every previously visited site is a different rule from forbidding immediate reversal. These finite counts prove no asymptotic bounds $2.6^n$ or $2.8^n$, and do not decide what the source means by returning or which quantity is bounded.

For a finite positive diagonal matrix $B$, put $H=\operatorname{diag}(B^2,I)$ and $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\-B^2&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Direct multiplication gives $A^*H+HA=0$: the matrix is skew-adjoint in that energy inner product. The quadratic quantity $Q(\varphi,\omega)=\|B\varphi\|^2+\|\omega\|^2$ satisfies $Q(cz)=|c|^2Q(z)$, rather than the degree-one homogeneity of a norm. Neither observation adds a square on the left or replaces the written word “self-adjoint”. An unbounded operator would also require domain analysis, which this finite calculation does not provide.

If a differentiable solution family satisfies $u'=Au+K(u)$, differentiation in the family parameter gives $v'=Av+DK(u)[v]$. For example, $K(u)=u^3$ gives $DK(u)[v]=3u^2v$, since
\[
\frac{(u+\varepsilon v)^3-u^3}{\varepsilon}
=3u^2v+3\varepsilon uv^2+\varepsilon^2v^3.
\]
The source's abbreviated $K_{u(\cdot)}$ is retained without supplying a factor. Similarly, a zero commutator of two undotted fields cannot also equal a nonzero Dirac distribution, or its derivative, under the usual interpretation. This conditional consistency observation does not establish a dot, prime or argument in the handwritten line.

For a measurable map $f$ and $\nu=f_*\mu$, a set in the completion of $\nu$ lies between measurable sets $E\subset A\subset F$ with $\nu(F\setminus E)=0$. Pulling back these inclusions shows that $f^{-1}(A)$ belongs to the completion of $\mu$, with the same completed measure. This proves the forward implication under explicit completion assumptions, without reconciling the source's A/B or X/Y.

The stated example can be explained conditionally as follows. Assume $X\subset Y=[0,1]$ is non-Lebesgue-measurable and has outer Lebesgue measure one. Every Borel subset of $Y\setminus X$ then has measure zero. Consequently $\mu(X\cap E)=\lambda(E)$ is well defined on trace-Borel sets: two representatives differ by a Borel null set. \subsection*{Hilbert sums, infinitesimal deformations and continuity (source frames 16--20)}
These comparisons state additional assumptions; they do not replace manuscript readings.

For $f\in L^2(\Omega)$ and a smooth compactly supported test function $\varphi$,
\[
T_f(\varphi)=\int_\Omega f\varphi,\qquad
|T_f(\varphi)|\leq\|f\|_2\|\varphi\|_2
\leq\|f\|_2\,|K|^{1/2}\sup_K|\varphi|
\]
when $\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset K$ with $K$ compact. Thus the functional is continuous on every fixed-compact test-function space, as required for a distribution. This supports a possible $\mathcal D'$ interpretation; it does not establish a prime in the heavy handwritten mark, and does not identify the topology intended on $E$.

Suppose $H_1,H_2$ are continuously included in a Hausdorff topological vector space $E$. The map $J:H_1\oplus H_2\to E$, $J(u,v)=u+v$, has closed kernel $N$. The quotient Hilbert norm is
\[
\|h\|_{\mathrm{quot}}^2
=\inf_{u+v=h}\bigl(\|u\|_{H_1}^2+\|v\|_{H_2}^2\bigr).
\]
Indeed, each affine fibre is a translate of the closed subspace $N$; its unique element orthogonal to $N$ minimizes the direct-sum squared norm. The induced map $(H_1\oplus H_2)/N\to E$ is injective by definition of $N$. This explains the triangular diagram conditionally, without supplying its lower indices. For $H_1=H_2=\mathbb C$ with ordinary norm, the minimum is $|h|^2/2$, attained at $u=v=h/2$. The printed diplomatic formula retains the missing visible square and the source comma in the subscript.

For $\lambda,\mu>0$, the scalar identity
\[
\frac{|x|^2}{\lambda}+\frac{|h-x|^2}{\mu}
=\frac{|h|^2}{\lambda+\mu}
+\frac{\lambda+\mu}{\lambda\mu}
 \left|x-\frac{\lambda h}{\lambda+\mu}\right|^2
\]
explains $(\lambda+\mu)H=\lambda H+\mu H$ under the stated reciprocal scaling of the scalar product; the same completed-square argument works with Hilbert norms. The inclusion order gives $\|h\|_{H_2}\leq\|h\|_{H_1}$ and $H_1+H_2\geq H_1$ by the decomposition $h=h+0$. Strict positivity of a norm requires $h\ne0$. Likewise $\lambda H\geq H$ is equivalent to $\lambda\geq1$ for a nonzero $H$; at $H=0$ the comparison holds for every $\lambda\geq0$. These qualifications do not amend the source inequalities.

The phrase about a common modulus requires care with quantifiers. On $[0,1]$, give $f_n(t)=nt$ probability $2^{-n}$ for $n\geq1$. Every sample function is continuous, and the law is carried by a countable union of finite compact sets. Every set of probability one nevertheless contains all the atoms $f_n$. For any fixed $\delta>0$, their increments $n\delta$ are unbounded. Thus that full-measure family has no single finite deterministic modulus tending to zero. This distinguishes a deterministic common modulus from individual or random moduli; it does not silently reinterpret the abbreviated source sentence.

For a differentiable family of matrix representations $\rho_t$, set
$u(g)=\dot\rho_0(g)\rho_0(g)^{-1}$. Differentiating
$\rho_t(gh)=\rho_t(g)\rho_t(h)$ gives
\[
u(gh)=u(g)+\operatorname{Ad}_{\rho_0(g)}u(h).
\]
For a conjugating family $\rho_t(g)=a_t\rho_0(g)a_t^{-1}$ with
$a_0=I$ and $v=\dot a_0$, one obtains
$u(g)=v-\operatorname{Ad}_{\rho_0(g)}v$. This identifies the elementary cocycle and coboundary calculation behind the $H^1$ discussion. It does not prove global rigidity, reconstruct the group hypothesis, or change the written ``not discrete''.

Finally, in a finite-dimensional positive-operator cone, $A=B+C$ determines $C=A-B$ uniquely when the difference is positive. This is cancellation in an ordered linear space. It is distinct from the usual geometric assertion that every boundary segment is trivial: in dimension three, the distinct positive trace-one matrices $\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0)$ have a whole segment of singular matrices between them. The source's terminology ``strictly convex'' is preserved. No general Hilbert-kernel representation theorem, universal-measurability assertion or nuclear-space claim is proved by these elementary comparisons.
