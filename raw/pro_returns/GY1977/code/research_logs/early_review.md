## Verdict

**The finite-jet propositions and their proofs are mathematically sound under the stated trivial, discrete coefficient conventions. No defect requiring a change to the conclusions was found.** In particular, the argument proves all torsion in integral degree two, not merely a visible two-dimensional subspace of the elements killed by2. The closed-commutator theorem is sufficient; no assertion about the unclosed abstract commutator subgroup is needed. The explicit primitive and its sign are correct for composition `f∘g=f(g(t))` and the stated inhomogeneous coboundary.

One useful citation improvement is to include **Babenko–Bogatyi, Proposition2.9, pp.249–250**, next to the assertion that the kernel of the explicit quotient map is the closed commutator. Proposition2.1 supplies the surjective homomorphism, while Proposition2.9 explicitly identifies its kernel. The development already cites the abelianization theorem, so this is a locator refinement rather than a logical gap.

## Cochains, finite stages and coefficient exactness

The quotient kernels `f(t)≡t mod t^(N+1)` are open normal subgroups, and the truncation maps are surjective. Thus the inflation maps on ordinary cochains are injective, so their union is a legitimate subcomplex of all cochains on J.

Any finite collection of cochains in this union has a common stage. A cocycle condition holds at that stage because the projection to the finite-jet group is surjective; a coboundary relation involving a higher-stage primitive holds at a common larger stage. This verifies the stated filtered-colimit formula. It does **not** require the inflation maps on cohomology to be injective, and the proof does not accidentally assume they are.

For a surjection of coefficient groups B→C, a C-valued cochain factoring through J_N^r lifts by choosing a B-valued lift for each value of its function on J_N^r. The lift continues to depend on the same jet. This proves degreewise surjectivity and the coefficient long exact sequence. In particular, there is no hidden bounded-denominator requirement in the passage from Q/Z to Q: J_N is generally infinite, and the allowed cochains may have infinitely many values. A choice of rational representative for each value is permitted.

The source's uniform-local-constancy condition is equivalent to factoring through one jet because the open normal jet kernels form a neighborhood base. Ordinary continuity of an arbitrary higher-degree cochain need not provide a common jet. The development properly preserves that distinction. In degree one, homomorphisms are special: continuity to a discrete target makes the kernel open, hence contains one jet kernel. Therefore every continuous homomorphism does factor through a common finite jet.

## Degree one and the use of the closed commutator

Let C be the closure of the subgroup generated by commutators, and let A0=J/C. The primary theorem and explicit quotient map identify A0 with Z²⊕(Z/2)², with the quotient map already factoring through the seventh jet.

A finite-jet homomorphism J→D into a discrete abelian group has closed kernel containing the commutators, hence contains C. It therefore factors through A0. Conversely, every homomorphism A0→D yields a finite-jet homomorphism by composition with (J1). This gives the claimed identification of H¹_jet(J;D) with Hom(A0,D).

No continuity assumption on an arbitrary abstract homomorphism out of J is inferred. The result is restricted to finite-jet H¹, as required. Taking D=Z leaves exactly the two free coordinates x=a and y=b−a².

## All torsion in integral degree two

The exact coefficient sequence gives the segment

Hom(A0,Q) → Hom(A0,Q/Z) → H²_jet(J;Z) → H²_jet(J;Q).

Here

- Hom(A0,Q)=Q²;
- Hom(A0,Q/Z)=(Q/Z)²⊕(Z/2)²;
- the first map surjects onto the first two factors and is zero on the last two.

Hence its cokernel is precisely (Z/2)². Exactness identifies this cokernel injectively with the **entire kernel** of the map from integral degree two to rational degree two.

The rational cochains form a complex of Q-vector spaces, since pointwise rational scalar multiplication preserves finite-jet dependence. Its cohomology is consequently a Q-vector space and has no nonzero torsion. Every integral torsion class lies in the computed kernel. Conversely, that kernel is finite of exponent2, so every element of the kernel is torsion. This proves

Tor H²_jet(J;Z) ≅ (Z/2)².

The argument rules out odd torsion and elements of order4 or higher. It does not use a universal-coefficient theorem or require integral cohomology to commute with tensoring by Q. Those would introduce unnecessary hypotheses; the direct long exact sequence is sufficient.

The mod2 Bockstein calculation mentioned later is a useful consistency check but is correctly described as insufficient by itself to exclude odd or higher torsion.

## The displayed torsion cocycles

The two characters εi/2 take values in Q/Z. Lift each by Ei/2 with Ei∈{0,1}. For trivial coefficients, the coboundary of this lift is

Bi(f,g)=(Ei(f)+Ei(g)−Ei(f∘g))/2.

Since εi is additive modulo2, the numerator is even, so Bi is integer valued. It depends on the seventh jet, satisfies δBi=0 and obeys2Bi=δEi. The two characters are independent on the torsion summand of A0, and no nonzero such character lifts to a rational-valued homomorphism there. Their connecting classes are therefore independent and of exact order2. This verifies both the representatives and the nontriviality argument.

The development makes no claim that these classes remain nontrivial after passing to completely unrestricted discrete cochains. Such a claim would need separate work, since new non-finite-jet primitives could matter. Its discrete-cohomology statement is confined to the explicit degree-one product primitive, which is valid there directly.

## Independent derivation of the product primitive and sign

For f=t+at²+bt³+ct⁴+… and g=t+At²+Bt³+Ct⁴+…, direct expansion gives

a(f∘g)=a+A,

b(f∘g)=b+B+2aA,

c(f∘g)=c+C+aA²+2aB+3bA.

Substituting into w=c−3ab+2a³ yields

w(f∘g)=w(f)+w(g)+aA²−aB.

Consequently δw(f,g)=w(g)−w(f∘g)+w(f)=a(B−A²)=x(f)y(g), exactly as claimed. This expansion was re-derived independently rather than inferred from the parent's check output.

For the second verification, z=c+a³−2ab has composition law

z(f∘g)=z(f)+z(g)+(b−a²)A.

Thus `(x,y,z)(X,Y,Z)=(x+X,y+Y,z+Z+yX)`, an integral Heisenberg law. The inverse coordinate change is b=y+x² and c=z+x³+2xy, with integral coefficients, so it is a genuine integral coordinate identification. Since w=z−xy, the alternative primitive derivation agrees with the direct calculation.

There is also a direct check of the reversed product: δz(f,g)=−y(f)x(g). The squares satisfy δ binom(u,2)=−u(f)u(g) for every integer-valued characteru. These formulas alone show all products of the two specified degree-one generators vanish, even without invoking graded commutativity.

## Scope and recommended disposition

The development has the correct bounded scope: degree one, all torsion in degree two, explicit torsion representatives, and vanishing products of the specified degree-one generators in the chosen integral finite-jet theory. It does not compute the torsion-free part of degree two or higher integral groups, and it does not identify unrestricted continuous or discrete cohomology with finite-jet cohomology.

**Recommendation: retain the results and proof.** Add Proposition2.9 as the exact closed-kernel locator if convenient. For maximum terminological precision, “discrete trivial coefficient group” can be written “discrete abelian group with trivial action,” although that convention is already implicit in ordinary group-cohomology notation. Neither refinement changes the argument or conclusions.
