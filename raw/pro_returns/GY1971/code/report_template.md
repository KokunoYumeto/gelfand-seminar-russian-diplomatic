## 3. Findings that materially changed the reading

**Prior circulation changes the K-theory chronology.** Quillen's 1970 ICM account, printed in 1971, already gives the stable plus construction, Brauer lifting, the Adams-operation fibre theorem, the finite-field computation and the algebraic-closure/Frobenius statement. Its second section gives the finite-GL cohomology formula with the order of the field cardinality modulo the coefficient prime. The 1972 Annals paper is the later detailed publication, not evidence that the seminar's 1972 reports preceded public announcement. Matching these actual statements changed the corresponding occurrences to E (Quillen, 1971, §§2–3; 1972).

**Pták's rational-parameter uncertainty was already outdated.** Kirzhner and Tabachnikov's theorem for algebraic unit spheres and their rational-p corollaries appeared in the May–June 1971 issue, before the September talk. This is a stronger chronological finding than a later paper with the same topic. It does not settle the exact questions about irrational parameters, the minimum over all norms in a given dimension, or the full constrained extremal problem. Those current fates remain explicitly unverified within this run (Kirzhner & Tabachnikov, 1971, Theorem 1 and Corollaries 2–3).

**A later account can identify a rough number without verifying its literal wording.** The approximately eighty-million determinant-one forms on P000398 match the classical lower bound for positive-definite even unimodular integral lattices of rank 32. Restoring those hypotheses gives 80,618,466 as a lower bound, not an exact enumeration. King prints that value and a substantially stronger lower bound. The identification is strong but reconstructed because the notebook does not supply all the genus conditions (King, 2003, §§2, 6).

**The March Vaserstein announcements have a near-contemporary source.** A search directed only to the long 1976 *Foundations* paper would obscure the two-page 1973 announcement, received 14 November 1972. That announcement and the detailed 1976 comparison identify the solution of the elementary-relations question raised earlier in the same seminar sequence. The comparison also distinguishes a doubled fibre product from a bare kernel and avoids silently assuming unrestricted excision for arbitrary nonunital ideals (Vaserstein, 1973, p. 231; 1976, §29, Theorem 29.1).

**Absence of zero divisors is insufficient for a K-theory comparison.** P000422 visibly gives that hypothesis for agreement with Karoubi's theory. The graded cusp domain F_p[t²,t³] contracts polynomially to F_p, so its second Karoubi–Villamayor group vanishes. Hesselholt and Nikolaus's relative algebraic K-group calculation, together with the split augmentation, shows that its ordinary K₂ is nonzero. The occurrence contains the full diagnostic deduction, marked as editorial work and independently checked for every prime, including 2 and 3. This uses a later published result to test the literal hypothesis; it makes no priority claim for the counterexample (Hesselholt & Nikolaus, 2020, Theorem A).

**Two attractive topological readings fail without their qualifications.** The Godbillon–Vey class is not invariant under arbitrary deformation of a foliation; Thurston's varying examples make the distinction from foliated concordance decisive. Likewise, the full de Rham operator anticommutes with degree parity and has zero spectral flow around a parity-preserving loop. A potentially nontrivial signature example requires additional structure absent from the literal notation. Neither correction invalidates the general spectral-flow/index theorem (Thurston, 1972; Atiyah et al., 1976, §7).

**The exceptional eta exponent is not a surviving mystery.** The notebook's leading-q omission is independently visible from the constant term and from Dyson's original formula. The separate question about exponent 26 has a later root-system explanation involving the dual-root construction for F4. It is retained as a historical problem with that fate, rather than converted into a new open question (Dyson, 1972, pp. 636–637; Monastyrskii, 1980, pp. 200–201).

**Morava's circulation and publication require separate labels.** The two-page 1972 moduli announcement is an unpublished document, communicated on 22 November. Johnson and Wilson's 1975 paper separately cites Morava's unpublished Columbia notes from 1971. The chapter distinguishes the formal-group height picture from later constructions of extraordinary K-theories, and distinguishes an ordinary module fibre from a homology theory. It does not treat an unpublished note as a 1972 journal article (Morava, 1972; Johnson & Wilson, 1975).

**A title match does not supply a classification theorem.** Carey and Pincus's 1976 paper directly addresses unitary equivalence modulo trace class. Its bibliographic identification is secure, but this run did not obtain and read the full two-operator classification criterion. The chapter states the precise bounded-operator question, the necessary absolutely-continuous-part condition, the dedicated later literature and this verification limit. It does not infer an exact necessary-and-sufficient criterion from the paper's title (Carey & Pincus, 1976).

## 4. Search method, difficult points and corrections

Research was split into three contiguous record groups and reconciled centrally. The Russian transcription controlled formula interpretation; the English text was a reading aid. Decisive scans were opened for boundaries, signs, coefficients, overwritten or uncertain words and claimed mathematical errors. The three group ledgers in `audit/` identify the scans actually inspected and the sources actually opened. A source-page coverage row is not represented as a claim that every mark on every scan received independent palaeographic verification.

The productive search paths were Math-Net's short Soviet announcements and linked full texts, including its Russian translation of Kostant; Numdam's primary papers; accessible primary texts by Griffiths, Birman, Atiyah–Patodi–Singer and Vinogradov; Ravenel's archive for Morava and Johnson–Wilson; and backward references to Quillen's ICM announcement. Exact theorem locators mattered more than topical similarity. The finite affine Radon calculation, for example, has a related later projective finite-ring paper, but that relation is not an exact publication match for the affine range theorem.

Searches combined names, dates, English and Russian terms, characteristic formula fragments and bibliographic references. The ledgers retain representative queries and unsuccessful retrievals. For Pták's minimum-exponent question, the search also distinguished an apparently relevant 1995 “exponential numbers” invariant from the notebook's contraction exponent. The invariants have different quantifiers; transferring a bound between them would have been an unjustified solution claim. Search failure is treated as a verification limit, never as proof of nondevelopment.

@@CORRECTION_TABLE@@

The CSV distinguishes **transcription**, **translation**, **ambiguity**, **faithful_error** and **provenance**. A faithful mathematical error is an emendation of what the notebook actually says; it is not charged to its transcriber. Examples include the missing leading factor in the eta product, a bare fixed subgroup being identified with maps into a homotopy fibre, and inappropriate universal exactness or coefficient assumptions. Ambiguity is retained where the scan does not warrant a unique replacement, including Bott's threshold prose versus its strict formula. Provenance entries correct boundaries and dates without rewriting the mathematical source.

The most difficult work was determining the exact object behind shorthand: whether a plane is an affine hyperplane, whether a K-theory is algebraic or homotopy invariant, whether a quadratic form means a diagonal refinement or its hermitianization, whether an orbit is taken over a fixed algebraically closed field, and whether a purported fibre retains a null-homotopy. The modern statement supplies such hypotheses explicitly and the reconstruction field identifies the supply. An F label is used when those choices cannot be made securely enough to identify the intended claim.

## 6. What the six classes miss and proposed format changes

The classes are useful retrieval labels but combine several independent questions. E can contain a theorem first announced only shortly before the seminar, a classical construction or a later exposition of a prior result. A has no specified maximum interval for “soon after”: a 1973 short announcement and a 1976 full treatment should not collapse into one date. B does not itself indicate an exact theorem match. F can mean an absent theorem, an unidentified bibliographic lead, or a mathematical assertion whose missing hypotheses admit incompatible readings. C in this chapter records partial verified development and an explicit current-fate verification gap; it must not be read as certification of 2026 openness.

The existing correctness, match, verification and confidence fields mitigate these problems and should remain visible. Additional structured fields would make the record safer:

| Proposed field | Purpose in this year |
| --- | --- |
| `catalogue_date`, `source_date`, `critical_date`, `date_basis` | Preserve the Dobrushin correction and the unresolved February date discrepancy without losing either witness. |
| `source_segments` with page and start/end anchor | Represent embedded interventions and genuine boundaries, rather than broad overlapping page windows. |
| `attribution_basis` and `attribution_confidence` | Make record 257's conditional attribution and named interventions explicit. |
| `first_circulation`, `publication_date`, `publication_kind` | Separate congress announcements, received dates, unpublished notes and final journal publications. |
| `result_locator` and `source_access_level` per reference | Distinguish reading a theorem from opening a metadata page and allow several sources to support different parts of one item. |
| `status_evidence_date` and `frontier_verified` | Prevent a dated audit with incomplete current-status evidence from masquerading as a verified open problem. |
| `relation_type` for cross-item links | Distinguish identical question, exposition, correction, consequence and thematic relation. |
| `core_scope` and `context_only` | Keep the requested 1973 outlier and adjacent unrequested pages from distorting a year view. |

These are recommendations; the seven delivered data files retain the supplied schema. Additional audit CSVs carry useful provenance without making the reader depend on an incompatible data format. The cumulative bibliography contained repeated keys; selected entries were deduplicated while retaining established keys, and the merge audit records the affected keys and any aliases.

## 7. Presentation on the companion website

A reader arriving at a talk should first see its date and source evidence, exact page segments, actual speakers or interventions, and a one-paragraph mathematical map. The next view should be the occurrence list with a concise modern statement and a visible distinction between the notebook and reconstruction. Fate and present status should be adjacent, with their verification level and exact source locator visible before a reader follows a bibliographic link. A verified URL badge alone is insufficient.

The strongest navigation paths in this chapter are the elementary-relations question from record 247 to its answer in 253; the finite-field sequence from 258 through 259 and the site/cohomology material in 265; the Vinogradov sequence 240–242; the bordism/formal-group sequence 268 and 270; and the foliation material's link to the preceding year's GP-1970-26. The Dobrushin record should display a date-correction banner and a link into the later academic year, while remaining reachable from this explicitly requested packet. Headings and the reading plan should be navigable without appearing in mathematical-item totals.

Problem pages should show all occurrences in chronological order, retain solved historical questions, and link each answer to the exact matched theorem rather than to a broad author bibliography. A correction view should put the scan, Russian wording, English rendering and critical reading side by side. “Faithful mathematical error” should remain visibly distinct from “transcription error.” Search should index normalized formulas as well as names and titles, but keep the literal notation available: q versus a prime coefficient, algebraic versus homotopy-invariant K-theory, and full versus strict coordinate changes carry mathematical content.

## 8. Reproducibility and build

The top level contains the seven schema files, this report, the generated chapter, its test driver, the compiled PDF and the supplied builder/style. The frozen source fragments and authoring scripts are included under `code/`. The final data files are the editorial source of record. To reproduce the data merge from the frozen fragments, run `python code/merge_fragments.py`; to regenerate the statistics and overview, run `python code/make_report.py`. Then run `python code/audit_data.py` and `python code/build.py`.

The build wrapper executes the supplied `python build_chapter.py GY1971`, followed by **XeLaTeX → BibTeX → XeLaTeX → XeLaTeX**. This environment had TeX input trees but lacked the generated XeLaTeX format and usable filename database. A private format was generated in the workspace and the input trees were searched directly; no system installation was changed. The wrapper reproduces that repair when necessary. Four existing DejaVu Serif font faces and their license are bundled to provide consistent Latin and Cyrillic rendering using the supplied style's existing font-path support.

Machine consistency checks cover record coverage, sequential academic-year identifiers, required fields and enumerations, reference keys, problem backlinks and status dates. The source audit separately checks all 99 page dispositions; these checks are not proofs of historical or mathematical correctness. Build logs, the final consistency output, computational outputs and the PDF layout inspection record are in `audit/`. The final PDF was rendered and visually inspected, including the overview, long formulas, Russian quotations, development, problem index, corrections and bibliography; the layout audit records the inspected pages and remaining nonfatal warnings, if any.
