## 2. Search method and levels of evidence

Searches used combinations of exact article titles, authors, formula keywords, DOI metadata and publisher or author repositories. Primary sources were opened before their content was used. The item fields distinguish exact, partial and thematic matching, and distinguish reading an original result from verifying metadata only. A later review may explain an old theorem but is not by itself evidence of first circulation.

Representative query families were: Makeenko Migdal exact equation loop average 1979; large N Wilson loop factorization normalization; Elitzur local gauge symmetry; Barrett reconstruction holonomy 1991; Kazhdan Lusztig Schubert varieties Poincare duality 1979 preprint; Vogan Ext parity 1979; Gabber Joseph Kazhdan Lusztig conjecture; Olshanskii infinite dimensional classical groups finite rank 1978; construction representations classification 1980 Doklady 250; MacPherson combinatorial Pontrjagin classes; Gelfand MacPherson Grassmannians dilogarithm 1982; Rojas Grassmannian polylogarithms 2026; Zakharov Benney equations conservation laws kinetic equation; Gelfand Gindikin Shapiro local integral geometry curves; admissible complexes curves Gelfand Graev; and L2 intersection homology Zucker Looijenga Saper Stern.

## 3. Migdal: loop calculus, normalization and confinement

**Makeenko and Migdal (1979).** Primary article, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(79)90131-X . Publisher/Crossref metadata was initially checked; a full author-uploaded copy was then opened at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222390357_Exact_equation_for_the_loop_average_in_multicolor_QCD . The upload is explicitly attributed to Yu. Makeenko (15 February 2020). The original article states receipt on 17 September 1979 and publication on 3 December 1979. Equations (12), (14), (16), (18), (19) and (20)–(23) were read for the area derivative, covariant path derivative, color contraction/Schwinger–Dyson equation, finite-color loop relation, normalized large-color factorization and lattice form. These locators establish pre-talk availability; the 2000 review is supplementary exposition.

**Makeenko (2000).** https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0001047 and the full primary author PDF were read. Relevant locators are §3.4, Eqs. (109)–(110); §4.2, Eqs. (135)–(136); §4.3, Eqs. (147), (150), (157)–(161); and §4.6 on regularization. They support the precise normalization, the distinction between the finite-color hierarchy and its factorized limit, and the need for gauge-covariant connectors under smearing. The arbitrary-path translation assertion is kept as a qualified partial match rather than falsely attributed to an exact numbered 1979 formula.

**’t Hooft (1974).** The full primary author PDF of “A planar diagram theory for strong interactions,” DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(74)90154-0 , was read, especially §3 and its Euler-characteristic counting. The original records receipt on 21 December 1973 and publication on 18 April 1974. This supports the chronology of the large-color topological expansion and its conventional normalization; it does not prove continuum confinement.

**Wilson (1974), Elitzur (1975), Barrett (1991).** Existing cumulative keys were reused for Wilson and Elitzur. Wilson's DOI is https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.10.2445 . Elitzur's primary publisher abstract/metadata at https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.12.3978 was checked, but the full APS harvest was inaccessible (401), so the corresponding item explicitly says metadata verification and partial matching. The literal assertion that every gauge-noninvariant function has zero average is false: only its noninvariant component is killed by gauge averaging. A gauge-invariant component may remain. Barrett's correct holonomy-reconstruction DOI is https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00671007 ; the abstract/metadata were read, not the proof. A superficially returned DOI ending BF00674341 was unrelated and discarded.

**Chatterjee.** https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.07719 and the full primary text were read, especially Theorem 3.1 and Corollaries 3.2–3.3. The first version is dated 26 February 2015; the revised text is dated 14 December 2017. The rigorous string expansion, factorization and area-law consequences concern strong-coupling lattice SO(N) gauge theory. They do not settle the exact continuum Yang–Mills confinement problem in the notebook. The Clay/Jaffe–Witten statement is cited under its pre-existing cumulative key for the general unresolved continuum goal. The proposed large-color route remains a separate method item, with all limits and loop normalizations stated.

## 4. Zelevinsky and the first MacPherson fragment: KL and intersection homology

The original proof was not read in this pass; a GDZ route exposed only a JavaScript shell. The notebook's canonical basis is identified with Theorem 1.1 and the multiplication/cell formula with Theorem 1.3 without claiming a new proof. Missing distant commutations and the replacement of strict by nonstrict Bruhat order are recorded as corrections visible in the scan.

**Vogan (1979).** The full primary journal copy of “Irreducible characters of semisimple Lie groups II: The Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures,” https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/vogan/vogan-IC2-duke-version.pdf , was read, particularly Conjectures 3.3–3.4 and Theorem 3.5 on pp. 809–810. The Ext parity and its connection with the character formula are separate claims. The paper's 1979 chronology precedes the seminar; the conjecture's eventual proof is distinguished from that earlier formulation.

**Gabber and Joseph (1981).** Full primary PDF: https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.1406.pdf . The introduction on pp. 261–262 was read for the precise Ext formula and the character conjecture. The footnote on p. 261 explicitly says that Kazhdan–Lusztig had solved the relevant geometric problem. Reference 11 on p. 301 identifies “Schubert Varieties and Poincare Duality, preprint, Harvard, 1979.” This independently verified circulation evidence is decisive: the geometric identification on P000907 is classified E, not a later 1980 solution merely because the proceedings volume bears 1980. The article records receipt on 28 September 1980 and revision on 20 February 1981; the Luminy meeting was 16–20 June 1980.

**Kazhdan and Lusztig (1980).** “Schubert varieties and Poincare duality,” Proceedings of Symposia in Pure Mathematics 36, 185–203, DOI https://doi.org/10.1090/pspum/036/573434 . Primary institutional/publisher metadata was opened. The full proof was not read. Its published year and the independently witnessed 1979 Harvard preprint are stated separately.

## 6. MacPherson: flattenings, Pontryagin classes and polylogarithms

**MacPherson (1978).** Full primary Bourbaki article: https://www.numdam.org/item/SB_1976-1977__19__105_0.pdf , pp. 105–124. The exposition was delivered in February 1977 and published in 1978. Sections 1–8 were read, especially §2 on configuration spaces; §3, Propositions 1–2 on the cocycle; §4 on the homological construction; and §6 on absolute Plücker coordinates and hypersimplices. These are exact contemporary matches for the structural construction in the seminar. The uncertain cell/stratification suggestion is classified F because the fragment does not specify a unique mathematical problem.

The flattening definition, face gluing, configuration quotient, low-codimension models, graph connectivity and weighted graph cycles were separately inventoried. The printed \(1/48\) normalization is retained with orientation hypotheses. The scan's codimension-index slip and \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) for four rays in the plane are recorded, the latter corrected to \(\mathbb{RP}^1\). The four-ray cross-ratio explanation is separated from a universal identification of all degenerate configurations. For the positive-scaling action, the effective orbit is the quotient by the full stabilizer, which can exceed common scaling for disconnected configurations.

**Gelfand and MacPherson (1982).** Primary metadata/Crossref record for “Geometry in Grassmannians and a generalization of the dilogarithm,” Advances in Mathematics 44(3), 279–312, June 1982, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-8708(82)90040-8 . The publisher full text was blocked (403); its proof was not read. Saved evidence: gelfandmacpherson1982metadata.json. This remains the chronological anchor for the later dilogarithmic construction.

**Gelfand and MacPherson (1992).** https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9204231 , first deposited 1 April 1992, DOI https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-1992-00282-3 . The primary abstract was read for the broader later Pontryagin-class construction, not used as a proof that every informal 1980 detail had already been established.

**Rojas (2026).** Juan Diego Rojas, “On explicit formulas for Grassmannian polylogarithms and Gelfand–MacPherson forms,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35754 , version dated 26 October 2026. The 42-page primary PDF was downloaded after the web reader failed; saved evidence is rojas2026.pdf and rojas2026.txt. Sections 3.4–3.7 and §3.9, Theorem 3.7, were read for the classical \(k=1\) construction, fiber compactification and real Rogers dilogarithm. This is supplementary confirmation of a classical construction and an author-claimed recent preprint, not a replacement for the 1982 chronology. No unverified general higher-polylogarithm assertion or exact orientation constant is imported. The parent received the arXiv ID, URL and precise locator for an independent check.

## 7. Zakharov: fluid reductions and extra conservation branches

**Zakharov (1980).** Primary MathNet record and full Russian original: https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/faa1795 , DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01086549 . Russian Functional Analysis and Its Applications 14(2), 15–24; English 89–98; received 17 December 1979. Section 2 was read for the infinite-fluid formulation, Hamiltonian bracket and kinetic equation; §4, Eqs. (48)–(52) and (58)–(59), for additional conservation branches and involution; and §5 for the multidimensional extension.

**Zakharov (1981).** Full primary author PDF: https://zakharov.itp.ac.ru/pub/data/1981/1981-04-1981_PD_Z.pdf , Physica D 3(1–2), 193–202, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(81)90126-3 . Equations (11)–(15) give the energy, bracket, delta-superposition distribution, height and Vlasov-type equation. Equations (35)–(39) give spectral branches, principal-value conventions and involution. The text explicitly does not claim proved completeness of the commuting integrals. The PDF and selected page PNGs are saved in evidence/.

The extra-branch reconstruction is stated first for a finite number of fluid layers, with \(R=p+\sum_a\rho_a/(p-u_a)\), and with the invertible local spectral branches specified. Periodic or decaying boundary conditions are stated before claiming conservation. A continuum principal-value limit requires additional hypotheses. The two-dimensional canonical formulation is confined to the potential-flow variables actually supported by the source; unrestricted vorticity is not silently inserted.

The vertical pressure sign on P000915 was checked in the scan and against the hydrostatic derivation: the sign convention yielding \(p=h-z\) requires the corrected pressure term. Neglect of the vertical acceleration means the entire vertical material acceleration, not merely its nonlinear terms. Height conservation includes the free-surface and bed boundary conditions. The kinetic analogy noted by Gelfand is separately classified as thematic.

**Vlasov.** Primary publisher material was opened at https://www.ufn.ru/en/articles/1968/6/a/ and https://www.ufn.ru/ru/articles/1967/11/f/ . DOI https://doi.org/10.1070/PU1968v010n06ABEH003709 . The 1968 English publication is a translation/reprint of the original 1938 work, not its first appearance. Metadata/contents were read, not a full proof of a direct relation with Benney's system. Benney's 1973 DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/sapm197352145 was independently metadata-checked and saved, but not cited because it was not needed for an exact fate claim.

**Integral geometry.** Both full Russian primary articles were read:

- Gelfand, Gindikin and Shapiro (1979), https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/faa1896 , DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01077241 ; full PDF through https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=faa&option_lang=eng&paperid=1896&what=fullt . Sections 1–3, Definitions 1–2 and Theorems 1–4 support regular incidence, point-supported functionals, admissible one-parameter incidence fibers and the local classification. The 14:4 reference has two authors, not the three authors of the 13:2 paper.

The real case gives the relevant closed-form periods with zero evaluation coefficient; nonzero local inversion must not be inferred merely from technical admissibility. Both original papers state this restriction. This distinction is a substantive correction, not a later modern preference.
