## Publication matches, qualifications, and chronology

**Residues.** Baum–Bott 1970 is identified from curated author bibliographies. The original proof was not retrieved. Bruzzo–Rubtsov, arXiv:0910.2019v4, §4.5 Proposition 4.6, was opened and read: it explicitly verifies the virtual bundle TX−L* for a section of TX tensor L. This explains the notebook expansion c(TX)/(1−rho). The isolated zeros of the extended section include those on the polar divisor. Real localization retains the signed Pfaffian.

**Matrix deformations.** Math-Net identifies Arnold’s 1971 paper, RMS 26(2), 29–43. Metadata and abstract were checked, not the complete proof. The centralizer formula and nine-parameter count are elementary reconstructions. The drawn starred family is not claimed uniquely recovered. “Final object” is stronger than versality and is corrected.

**Milnor–Orlik.** The 1970 Topology paper predates the lecture. Hertling–Mase’s primary full text, Theorem 6.4(a,b), was read for the exact monodromy divisor and Jacobian formulas. Notebook weights are reciprocal coordinate weights. Isolated critical point and reduced middle homology are essential.

**Torsion.** Ray–Singer’s complete original was eventually found at the University of Kentucky mirror. Introduction p.145 expressly says equality with combinatorial torsion was not proved; Definition 1.6 p.149 defines the analytic invariant. The notebook establishes circulation by 1 March 1971. The construction is E, an exposition of other authors’ circulating work, rather than A merely because publication came later. Equality is B: Cheeger’s 1977 announcement and Müller’s 1978 paper. Their metadata/announcement were checked, not complete proofs. Milnor’s Russian preprint translation was read in §§3 and 12. Cumulative key milnor1966torsion is reused. The lens complex requires a nontrivial character twist; the ordinary universal cover is not acyclic. Reciprocal torsion conventions are preserved.

**KdV and factorization.** The complete Zakharov–Faddeev English paper was read at the indicated equations; received 14 June 1971, it follows the talk. The published canonical angle is arg b, while reflection is b/a. The notebook PDE and Hamiltonian have inconsistent factors/signs. The paper expressly omits many analytic proofs; no global modern symplectomorphism theorem is inferred. Faddeev’s full 1966 note was read: the modified Green equation is assumed uniquely solvable, with a small-potential guarantee. Its historical exceptional-parameter caveat is not a present-day open inverse-scattering conjecture.

**Griffiths.** The exact 1969 Annals I and II citations were checked in the author’s institutional bibliography. A linked PDF was a different 1968 paper and was not used as the 1969 original. Other full-text attempts failed. The tube and pole-reduction identities are independently reconstructed from the notebook, with access limits explicit. Integral tube exactness does not require discarding torsion. Its kernel is an image; closed ambient cycles need not bound. Degree is ell q−n−1. A general numerator is not automatically in the Jacobian ideal.

**Real curves.** Arnold’s full Russian paper was read, including §1 and Varchenko’s acknowledgment. Received 22 March, it follows the 15 March talk. The mod-four result and branched-cover method are A. Rokhlin’s full note was read; received 29 December 1971 and published 1972, it proves Gudkov’s mod-eight M-curve congruence. This is solved, not an unresolved remainder of Hilbert’s sixteenth problem. The notebook’s unqualified “50 cases” is not promoted to a verified enumeration.

**Orbits and integral forms.** Rolewicz’s original metadata and institutional account were opened; the PDF was blocked. The notebook’s construction is unpacked with tail estimates and separability. For Serre, the French national union catalogue verifies the 1970 original and the English publisher record identifies Chapter V. The French PDF could not be fetched. Stabilization is ambiguous: even hyperbolic planes preserve parity; a common odd indefinite plane erases it by the classification theorem.

**Geronimus.** Both the two-page UMN announcement and detailed FAA article were opened and read. The first was received 28 August 1970 and published in issue 1 of 1971, before April; the longer article was received 22 September 1970. All smooth equivariant maps are morphisms, while jointly surjective submersion families are covers. The notebook conflates these. Exact primary locators support the finite-group equivalence, sheafification, BG comparison, smooth-vector sheaf and differentiable-cohomology comparison. Representation categories matter; no equivalence with every continuous Banach representation is inserted.

**Modularity.** BCDT’s original Introduction/Theorem A and Cremona’s authorized Chapters 2 and 3 were read. Here “Weil conjecture” means elliptic-curve modularity, solved in full in 2001. The scan’s y³ is retained: a smooth homogeneous cubic Y³=X³−g1 XZ²−g2 Z³ has rational point [1:1:0] and may be elliptic. It is not automatically a mistranscription. Reduction needs a minimal Weierstrass model; 2 and 3 remain exceptional. Integration first produces a curve isogenous to the intended elliptic target.

**Coherent states and uncertainty.** Perelomov’s full original/archival article, Cartier’s full 1968 exposition, Meyer’s full Bourbaki paper, Bourgain’s 1986 preprint, and Gröchenig–Malinnikova’s full 2013 article were opened. Perelomov was received 20 August 1970 and published February 1971: lattice threshold/deletion are E; 2002 is archival posting.

The scan expressly asks for a product. General orthonormal-basis existence is positively settled: Meyer wavelets bound the product, Bourgain even bounds each centered second moment. Regular Gabor covariance imposes a stronger restriction. The parent’s wavelet proof was independently checked through both moment substitutions and operator conjugations; both are correct. No particular Gram–Schmidt ordering is thereby identified or solved. The item remains 1970-228.8 / GP-1970-59. All three requested bibliography keys are present.

Other literal defects are recorded: odd theta characteristics are not half of 4^N; the extra inner i spoils the standard unitary displacement generator; induction uses the specific tautological central character chi(t gamma)=t, not an arbitrary nontrivial central weight; an unnormalized complex-shift Gaussian sum can have growing terms.
