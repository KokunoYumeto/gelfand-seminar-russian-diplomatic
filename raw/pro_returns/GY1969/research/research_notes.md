# Independent editorial research and mathematical checks

Access and status date: 2026-11-09. These notes distinguish material actually
read from leads. The final report gives aggregate limitations.

## Primary-source results read

- Janssens and Neeb, arXiv:1302.2535v1 (2013-02-11), Theorems 4.11 and
  4.15, pp. 26 and 28: bounded representations and their passage to gauge
  groups. Journal metadata at Utrecht University verifies IMRN 2015(18),
  9081–9137; early online publication 2014-12-01. The group theorem is
  numbered 4.16 in the published version; the chapter explicitly identifies
  arXiv numbering when using 4.15. No unbounded classification follows.
- Ismagilov (1976), Math-Net sm2860, English PDF, introduction and sections
  1–2: the stated irreducibility hypothesis is base dimension at least five.
  It does not answer an interval question.
- Ismagilov (1981), Math-Net faa1716, complete Russian two-page note,
  pp. 73–74: the energy representation is reducible in dimension one,
  including all smooth maps of the closed interval; the proof uses commuting
  left and right Brownian translations. This is an obstruction to that
  construction, not a nonexistence theorem for all representations.
- Janssens and Neeb (2024), publisher and complete introduction pp. 1–12:
  classification requires projective positive energy hypotheses and a
  spacetime action. Those extra hypotheses are absent from the 1969 notes.
- Meyer, Bourbaki no. 662: February 1986 lecture, published 1987,
  Astérisque 145–146, 209–223, section 2 Theorem 1: Schwartz wavelet basis.
- Bourgain, IHES/M/86/58 (November 1986), introduction and main theorem:
  general orthonormal bases can have uniformly bounded centered position
  and momentum second moments. Published in JFA 79(1), 136–143 (1988).
  The primary scan's extracted numerical threshold is ambiguous; no exact
  numerical threshold is asserted in the companion.
- Gröchenig and Malinnikova (2013), publisher PDF, pp. 115–116,
  Theorems 1.1 and 6.1: sharp distinction between unrestricted bases,
  regular Gabor systems, and stronger moments. This corroborates the
  scope of Bourgain's positive result without replacing the primary paper.

## Checks supplied in this run

The Bell-block counterexample is derived without numerical approximation.
The source assertion has no translation-invariance assumption at this point;
the example is periodic with period two, which is sufficient. All finite
marginals are faithful. The exact code compares definition-based partial
traces with an independent block formula, checks every four-site subset and
every ordered commutator, and repeats with a different parameter.

The full-support construction is checked by finite interpolation with a
diagonal tail bound, an independent commutant argument, and a separate
bump-current conjugation test. Its C0 discontinuity is established by an
explicit vacuum-coefficient countersequence. The norm-continuous support
corollary is checked by finite-evaluation classification and a separate bump
argument. The four-subspace deformation is proved by an open dense orbit and
checked independently by exact endomorphism linear equations. The wavelet
uncertainty identity is checked by change of variables and by conjugating
position and momentum with the dilation/translation unitary. None of these
annotations claims mathematical novelty.
