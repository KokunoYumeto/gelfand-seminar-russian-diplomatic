## Principal decisions and limits

### Kirillov

The local formula is an announced programme, with nilpotent and compact cases separated. Kirillov1968’s actual compact semisimple theorem uses the rho shift; it is not evidence for an unrestricted all-group orbit formula. The SO(3) test reveals three independent issues: an omitted outer trace in the distribution definition, a missing square in the exponential Haar density, and a non-Euclidean normalization hidden in the spherical measure. With half-angle tau and orbital measure dS/(4 pi lambda), the final character is correct. The arithmetic and angular integration are checked in `checks.py`. The catalogue’s future-talk list is covered in the talk summary and is not invented into further MCCME records.

### Gelfand–Fuchs

The 1967 paper is an exact source, not merely an analogue: it contains the ray quotient, the global linear-chart definition, frame-space universality, the cocountable-coordinate proposition, invariant-form cohomology, compact dual, and Hom(G,H)/H theorem. It was received 15 July1967, before these talks, but the talks are reporting Gelfand–Fuchs research published soon afterward; A is used consistently for this research sequence.

The 1968 elementary-object axioms are **stronger** than the handwritten axioms: nearby semigroup elements must be invertible and have an equivariant retraction to G. Its universal model consists of tuples with an invertible coordinate, not literally every embedding T→T^I. This matters even for linear maps: a combined injection may have all individual coordinates singular. The written weighted level set misses rays supported at a sufficiently high single coordinate. The “as for S²” phrase following GL(2) does not establish an identification U(2)/O(2)=S²; the latter is dimensionally false, while the SL(2) compact dual is S². The ambiguity is retained.

GP-1967-01 links the precise classifying programme and its stated universal constructions. It is answered in this specified category, not described as a still-open request for an “actual” ordinary classifying space.

### Interval maps and Feynman parameters

Boyce’s primary paper carries receipt 9 January1967, revision 1 December1967, and publication1969. Its bibliography explicitly records Huneke’s1967 announcement. Consequently the September notebook records circulating counterexamples: E rather than a new unresolved question or a result discovered only in1969. GP-1967-02 records the fixed-point question and the adjacent differentiable fixed/periodic statement. It does **not** add the different all-continuous common-periodic-point conjecture from later literature.

The exact C1 statement attributed to Schwartz is verified indirectly in the primary research paper Alikhani-Koopaei1998, Introduction pp269–270: f is C1, g continuous, fg=gf, and x=f(x)=g^n(x). Schwartz’s own publisher metadata was opened, but his original proof was not read. The item explicitly says indirect, and no claim is made for arbitrary merely differentiable maps. Russian “конечной кратности” is read in this dynamical context as finite period, not multiplicity of a root. The three-denominator Feynman formula lacks 2!; the standard simplex already supplies a decisive a=b=c=1 check.

### Interpolation

The exact named Dikarev–Matsaev and Mityagin primary texts were read. Mityagin distinguishes minimal and maximal symmetric ideals; the notebook’s two-axiom symmetry definition does not automatically replace all his hypotheses. The Lorentz-space formula requires the usual concavity/positivity condition on its parameter. The Dikarev–Matsaev target is L^{q,p}, and its optimality quantifies over an entire endpoint-operator class with the stated measure-space conditions.

**The most consequential later-fate finding is negative:** Sharpley1975 §3, pp122–125, explicitly disproves Semenov’s underlying norm-representation theorem and records the consequences for the interpolation criteria. The fundamental function can behave like sqrt(t) while a Boyd exponent is an endpoint; separability does not rescue the general assertion. The correct modern criteria involve dilation-operator norms and Boyd indices. The exact publication match is therefore separated from literal correctness. The valid Lp specializations, Hausdorff–Young, Paley’s Lorentz coefficient bound, and Hardy–Littlewood’s monotone-series criterion are separately retained. The notebook visibly writes “Пойя”; the exact formula and Semenov’s own identification support emending the name to Paley.

No exact early Semenov publication for the isolated Hilbert-intermediate-space assertion was identified in the1965/1967/1968 papers. Its qualified nonatomic symmetric-space version is justified directly by disjoint indicators and Hilbert type/cotype; attribution confidence is medium and verification indirect. The broad abstract representation question lacks a specified Banach couple/category and remains F.

### Sinai and Adler–Weiss

Adler–Weiss1967 is an **exact proof-level match**. Its §4 has the same four alphabet groups p,q,r,s, determinant ps−qr=1, and recoding to the all-ones N×N matrix with a single forbidden transition; §5 has the two-rectangle torus partition and connected component refinement. The paper was communicated24April1967 and printedJune1967. Hence the two-torus entropy theorem and special coding are E in October. GP-1967-03 joins the repeated precise metric-isomorphism question. Isomorphism means measure-preserving and invertible modulo zero, not an arbitrary measurable intertwiner or a global topological conjugacy.

Adler–Palais1965 proves affine rigidity for ergodic toral automorphisms. The translation by a fixed point cannot be dropped unless U(0)=0. The notebook’s arbitrary compact-abelian parenthesis is not uncritically generalized.

The Perron-vector star in the scan is real, not an OCR artifact. The written row-transition formula requires a right vector r, while stationarity requires w_i=l_i r_i. `checks.py` gives a nonsymmetric example where using a left vector produces row sums approximately .8393,1.2956,1.8393. The corrected formula has row sums1 and entropy log(lambda).

The parenthesis extending equal-entropy isomorphism to all primitive finite Markov models is distinguished from Adler–Weiss’s special proof. Ornstein’s primary author exposition, Russian1975, §5 Theorems4–5, explicitly combines the1970 mixing-Markov Bernoulli result and equal-entropy Bernoulli classification. That result was read in the exposition, not in the inaccessible original1970 proof. This gives B to the qualified extension.

Sinai’s first paper was received20October1967; his construction paper was received3November1967. The former announces small Markov partitions and develops coding and leaf measures; the latter proves existence. In particular, **Theorem5.1 on leaf measures is general in his “transitive” (=primitive-matrix) setting**, not restricted to the two-torus. Theorem5.2 then specializes to the torus. This was communicated to the agent treating Margulis166. Stable-leaf scaling is lambda^{-1}, not entropy; entropy is log(lambda). The first paper’s Theorem5.4 is specifically the Haar/Markov theorem for hyperbolic toral automorphisms in all dimensions.

The global compact-distance expansion inequality is impossible literally; derivative or intrinsic leaf metrics are needed. Nilpotence alone is not sufficient for a lattice-preserving Anosov automorphism: the Heisenberg center forces eigenvalue det(B)=±1. The only genuinely open item is the global algebraic classification, reusing GP-1966-01/1966-94.4. Its2026 status is checked in Damjanović’s primary ICM2026 article §2 as well as the exact formulation in Spatzier2016. The nonwandering-set margin note lacks the topology and genericity quantifier of a defined conjecture, so it is F.

### Leray

Leray1967 Theorem1 is the exact relative-period continuation of Nilsson. Its non-appui hypothesis must be restored. Algebraic singular support means a holomorphic multivalued function off an algebraic exceptional set; it does not mean the period is algebraic. A cycle on a complete intersection is not the whole intersection, and higher powers of dv in the residue notation are not ordinary exterior powers. Projective descent for differential forms requires horizontality as well as scale invariance.

Nilsson’s publisher explicitly gives **October1964** as the issue date; the familiar1965 volume citation is recorded as a bibliographic discrepancy. The bibliography key is `nilsson1964growth`. The full Nilsson proof was not read, but the primary PDF introduction and publisher metadata were opened, and Leray Corollary1.1 explicitly identifies its absolute-cycle theorem. Leray’s separate hypothetical slow-growth Theorem3 does not occur in the notebook and is not added as an open question.
