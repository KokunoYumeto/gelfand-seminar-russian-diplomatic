## Mathematical findings

### Matrix models and random surfaces

The cubic integral in record 682 is a formal Gaussian expansion. For a real nonzero cubic coupling it is not a convergent integral over all Hermitian matrices. The normalization in Brézin–Itzykson–Parisi–Zuber (1978), section 4, has quadratic term \(U^2/2\), while the notes first write \(U^2\). The later explicit series and critical constant therefore require rescaling or repair. Equation (52) gives \(g_c^2=1/(108\sqrt3)\), rather than the unsquared notebook expression. The genus power is \(N^{2-2g}\); the transcript’s use of an Euler-characteristic symbol in the exponent is also distinguished from genus.

The exact Ising constant in record 685 belongs to the cubic Boulatov–Kazakov model. Ambjørn–Sato–Tanaka (2020), equation (2.9), explicitly gives \(c_*=(2\sqrt7-1)/27=1/(2\sqrt7+1)\). This yields the recorded \(\beta_*=\frac12\log(2\sqrt7+1)\). Kazakov’s 1986 quartic-lattice paper is not used as an exact source for that cubic constant. The preprint/publication sequence is separated: Boulatov–Kazakov circulated in July 1986 and appeared in 1987.

In record 695, inverse-automorphism weights explain rational coefficients in the connected free energy. The printed \(k=100\) coefficient has 5-adic valuation \(-1\) and reduced denominator exactly \(5\); the saved exact calculation confirms the notebook’s nonintegrality observation. Merely labelling triangles need not destroy every residual symmetry, so the precise orbit–stabilizer reconstruction specifies a faithful labelling set.

The Gaussian embedding integral must remove the translational zero mode. A cofactor of the graph Laplacian then supplies the determinant factor; negative target dimension is a determinant continuation, not a literal negative-dimensional Euclidean space. The \(D=-2\) exact result is supported at the publication-abstract level by Boulatov–Kazakov–Kostov–Migdal (1986); the chapter does not claim an unread theorem uniform in target dimension.

The three-matrix angular variables satisfy \(U_{12}U_{23}U_{31}=I\). Integrating all three independently changes the model. Daul’s primary 1995 preprint identifies precisely this obstruction before its auxiliary-matrix construction. The scan also confirms a repeated final eigenvalue symbol in the notebook. The closure identity and corrected interpretation are explicitly marked as calculations supplied here.

### Compact unitary integrals and large-\(N\) gauge theory

The one-pair HCIZ integral is matched to Itzykson–Zuber (1980), equations (3.12)–(3.17), with normalized Haar measure, powers, factorials, and Vandermonde convention made explicit. The two-source integral is distinct. Collins–Śniady (2006), Corollary 2.4, provides an exact polynomial-moment method, hence an absolutely convergent finite-\(N\) exponential series on the compact group.

The two-source occurrences **1986-688.5 and 1986-695.11** link to the shared **GP-1986-60**, owned by the neighboring slice and consolidated by the root with 1986-698.1. There is no duplicate B problem object. Both B occurrences are class B with a partial publication match and are marked developed in the chapter. The root’s bounded development treats \(N=2\), the all-\(N\) series and remainder estimate, and a three-source obstruction.

The general three- and four-dimensional gauge problem remains separate as **GP-1986-31**. The original Eguchi–Kawai reduction needs the relevant unbroken center symmetry; quenching and related modifications do not amount to a general exact all-coupling continuum solution. García Pérez’s 2020 review and Neuberger’s 2020 analysis are used to define the scope accurately. A finite-dimensional moment expansion is not represented as a solution of this broader problem.

### KdV, super-KdV, and representation-dependent spectra

Ovsienko–Khesin’s manuscript was received August 8, 1986 and published in 1987. Proposition 1 gives the ordinary Euler interpretation; Proposition 2 supplies the super construction. The odd inertia involves an inverse derivative. The Neveu–Schwarz and Ramond cases differ at the odd zero mode: the former pairing is nondegenerate, while the latter has a degeneracy. These conditions matter and replace an overly vague statement about an invariant pairing.

The KdV quantization questions are split into three precise problems. **GP-1986-32** asks for commuting quantizations of the local integrals, resolved algebraically/formally and under the generic-parameter conditions in Feigin–Frenkel. **GP-1986-34** asks for the principal quantum Gelfand–Dickey/\(W_n\) construction. **GP-1986-33** asks for joint spectral and Jordan data in irreducible highest-weight modules; this is a stronger representation-specific problem.

Feigin–Frenkel’s *Integrals of motion and quantum groups* first circulated in October 1993 and its last arXiv version is dated September 1995, but the book chapter was published in **1996**, LNM 1620, pages 349–418. The bibliography key is **feiginfrenkel1996integrals**. The relevant result is Theorem 4.7.3 with section 4.7.1’s generic hypotheses; the \(W\)-algebra and BRST discussion is Theorem 4.6.9 and section 4.6.10.

### Modular lattices and representation theory

The three lattice problems that might otherwise be conflated are separated. Herrmann (1983), Theorem 1, concerns the word problem for the free modular lattice on four generators. Ryzhikova (1987) concerns decidability of linear identities over a fixed field and continues the earlier exact problem **GP-1985-20**. The current notebook’s finite-axiom question, under the explicit equational rational/all-field interpretation, is **GP-1986-35**. Herrmann (1984), Theorem 1.4, gives a pre-talk negative answer. The field and equational qualifiers are retained; arbitrary first-order axiom systems are not substituted silently.

The preprojective reduction announcement is **GP-1986-36**. Gelfand–Lidskii–Ponomarev’s 1987 paper, received December 9, 1986, supplies the rational large-rank description by compatible subspace families, eventual generation, and graded right ideals. The available OCR does not reliably distinguish the rank endpoint, and attempts at image access did not resolve it. The chapter therefore states only the safe range **\(r\ge7\)**. The status of \(r=6\) is uncertain only in this verification; no claim of mathematical openness is made. The paper’s remark that its assertion was unproved for \(r=5\) is explicitly a 1987 statement.

The scan confirms two literal definition errors: preprojectivity refers to finitely many source isomorphism classes with nonzero Hom, not finitely many morphisms over an infinite field; perfect elements are tested on indecomposable representations, not arbitrary direct sums.

For the general-linear unitary dual, exact primary statements were read in Tadić (1986) and Badulescu–Renard (2010), including Theorem 7.2 and the quaternionic normalization in Proposition 9.10. Vogan’s publisher dates his classification to October 1986. The complex local modulus is the square of ordinary complex modulus; the complementary interval \(0<\alpha<1/2\) uses that convention. Induction is normalized, discrete series means square integrability modulo the center, and the Speh segment exponents decrease by one. The scan confirms that the manuscript’s second exponent and missing last representation factor need mathematical repair. The phrase every field is restricted explicitly to the appropriate locally compact fields and representation categories.
