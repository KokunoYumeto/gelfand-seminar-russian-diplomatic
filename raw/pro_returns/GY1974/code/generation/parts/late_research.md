## The definite problem and rejected problem candidates

GP-1974-20 is the finite-alphabet inverse specification problem: given proper consistent exterior conditional kernels, determine the compatible probability measures, particularly existence and uniqueness. Its indexed occurrences are 1974-355.11 (the Dobrushin uniqueness criterion) and 1974-358.5 (the explicit inverse problem). Dobrushin's 1968 paper formulates this problem and proves quasilocal existence and contraction-based uniqueness. It is an established programme with model-dependent classification, not an isolated modern open conjecture.

The continuum DLR statements 355.3 and 355.7 are thematic precursors but are not put into the occurrence list of that finite-alphabet problem. This prevents changing the underlying configuration space without explanation.

Provisional GP-1974-21 was removed. The notebook's FIO recognition question does not state the order, uniformity over phase families, or symbol estimates needed to pose a definite mathematical characterization. The two occurrence entries 356.14 and 357.4 remain, cross-referenced to Hörmander's published testing framework. They have no problem ID.

The p-adic Gibbs and p-adic FIO suggestions likewise receive no problem IDs. The former does not specify whether the space, spin values, or probability values are p-adic. The latter does not specify symbols, canonical relations, or calculus operations. There is substantial relevant later literature, but an exact solution claim would be unjustified.

Bernstein's intervention is the Weyl-algebra dimension inequality, not the mixed-volume theorem. No link was forced to the middle group's Newton/Milnor-number problems GP-1974-12 or GP-1974-13.

## Mathematical checks and convention decisions

A single Fourier transform, exp(−ix·ξ), is used throughout the reconstructed microlocal statements. Upper-half-plane boundary values then have positive wavefront directions. The notebook's unspecified transform convention prevents calling its excluded-half-ray sign an unconditional error. Compact support is supplied for the global Fourier projection theorem, and primed kernel relations retain the minus sign on the input covector.

The phase-diagram count is a count of pure stable periodic phases under the Pirogov–Sinai hypotheses, not a finite count of every Gibbs measure. Distinct Gibbs measures have infinitely many convex mixtures. No general assertion excluding interface states is added. Finite interaction range is not confused with finite interaction order.

The two proofs in late_audit.tex are elementary audits:
- On T*(S1×R), constant-momentum leaves are Lagrangian cylinders. The restriction of the symplectic form, dimension, fundamental group, noncompactness, and completeness of the Hamiltonian fields were checked independently.
- For p=x1ξ1 at (0,0;1,0), dp=dx1 while Hp=−R. Homogeneous canonical transformations preserve R; scalar multiplication on the characteristic set preserves radiality; a coordinate covector has a nonradial Hamilton field. The two-dimensional stabilization avoids the incidental empty punctured characteristic set of a one-dimensional coordinate symbol.

These are marked as proved here but not claimed as new results. Root completed an independent review of both proofs and the Fourier-sign conclusion. The cylinder geometry, completeness, symplectic convention, stabilized radial obstruction, and negative-exponent Fourier sign all checked out; no correction to either proof was required.

For the complex-analytic calculations the reconstruction fixes ddc=(i/2π)∂bar∂ and ω0=(i/2)Σdzj∧dbarzj. The normalized counting/characteristic multiplier is π^(1−n), so the exact integrated identity is N+m=T+m(r0) with m the ordinary sphere average of log(1/||σ||). This was independently checked using the Euclidean divergence theorem:
∫B(t)ddc u∧ω0^(n−1)=(π^(n−1)/2)t^(2n−1) d/dt AvgS(t)u.
It reproduces the one-variable dt/t weight, while the notebook's t^(−n−1) does not.
