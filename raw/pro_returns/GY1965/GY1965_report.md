There is one class-C item, the unrestricted Weyl division-ring automorphism question. Its restricted rational-subfield stabilizer is developed completely, with known and newly supplied proofs distinguished from any novelty claim. There are no class-D entries. An underdetermined fragment is F even when an appealing modern problem could be invented from it. Conversely, a false literal assertion is not automatically F: when its intended standard theorem is identifiable, its fate and literal correctness are recorded separately.

## 5. Searches that materially changed the answer

1. **Atiyah–Bott chronology.** The opened Russian translation identifies the Harvard notes as autumn 1964. Its spectral-regularization sections, together with Bott's November 1965 Bourbaki report, establish that the long sequence is an exposition. They also prevent an erroneous correction: entire continuation for the transverse geometric trace is stronger than meromorphic continuation of a general spectral zeta function, and is valid in this special setting.

2. **Smirnov's exact short announcement.** Searching Math-Net by the positive-definite-function and nilpotent-group terminology located the 1966 Doklady note,170(3),524–525, received3 December 1965. The full two-page text was read. Its normalization, centrality and discrete characteristic-zero field are decisive. The matching title alone would have produced an overconfident A classification; the underdetermined notebook question remains F with a precise partial match.

3. **Coherent sheaves and K-theory.** Serre's full FAC paper and Borel–Serre's presentation of Grothendieck's Riemann–Roch theory supply the actual hypotheses. Finite presentation over an arbitrary ring cannot replace coherence; projective and coherent-sheaf Grothendieck groups require a resolution hypothesis for comparison. A missing unnamed ring in record66 remains unresolved even though these standard comparison theorems are known.

4. **Singularity and stability statements.** Boardman's original stratum theorems, Arnold's 1968 exposition, and the Mather material distinguish universal smooth jet strata from their possibly singular inverse images, smooth from topological stability, and formal from convergent or smooth equivalence. Later exact matches do not make every handwritten genericity statement literally correct. The explicit complex-square perturbation is followed as a resolved exercise, with its determinant and three ordinary cusps checked directly.

5. **Curvature-jet realization.** The original question about relations among covariant derivatives of curvature is matched to the metric-jet realization statement, with curvature symmetries, differentiated Bianchi identities and Ricci commutation constraints. The accessed Jentsch version is dated 13 January 2026; this is a source-version date, not an assertion that the underlying theorem originated in 2026. The chapter does not silently enlarge the question to a minimal presentation of every invariant ring.

6. **The torus intersection bound.** The graphical proof in the notes establishes the Morse case. The unqualified numerical bound fails without transversality. The later Hamiltonian statements are read with their actual assumptions; an endpoint exact-period condition is not silently asserted equivalent to a compactly supported Hamiltonian isotopy. Floer chronology is distinguished from the later exposition actually read. The explicit three-critical-point function is an independent check of the missing hypothesis.

7. **Gelfand–Kirillov's two growth notions.** The original 1966 French paper supplies both the logarithm omitted in the notebook and a different denominator-clearing invariant for division rings. The polynomial-subring PBW count alone cannot prove that the quotient division fields have different ranks. The chapter cites the actual division-ring theorem and keeps its base-field requirement.

8. **Alev–Dumas and the Weyl field.** A publisher preview containing the complete 1998 chapter gave the exact rational-subfield stabilizers and finite-group invariant-field theorem. Makar-Limanov's 1984 theorem concerns the polynomial algebra. Dumas's 2011 discussion and the recent Schwarz paper delimit, rather than solve, the unrestricted division-ring automorphism problem. A separate current-status check also located Lauritzen–Thomsen's November 2026 index-symmetry theorem; the exact corollary was read and its different scope recorded. Search leads from informal discussion were followed to primary sources and are not used as mathematical authority.

9. **Agranovich's calculus.** The1965 survey is a much closer source than generic later pseudodifferential textbooks. It fixes angular regularity thresholds, the omitted multiplication term, mapping orders, parametrix qualifications and index constructions. The latter do not require inventing new open problems from broad algebra remarks.

10. **Affine-plane integral geometry.** The1967 Gelfand–Graev–Shapiro article matches the closed-form range conditions, cycles and inversion coefficient. Restrictions on plane dimension and decay are explicit. A nonzero cycle coefficient is an existence statement, not a claim that all cycles invert the transform.

11. **Lidskii's all-zero zeta function.** The exact1967 Lidskii–Sadovnichii result explains why an entire all-zero continuation does not contradict the pole of the ordinary Riemann zeta function. The branch, multiplicities, removal of a zero at the origin and complete sectorial asymptotics are necessary parts of the match. The positive-zero half-sum is a different function.

12. **Gindikin's damaged operator name.** Independent inspection of P000138–139 reads “hyperbolic,” not “pseudo-.” This leads directly to the 1967 Doklady announcement, submitted 27 April 1966: the V-elliptic mapping, hyperbolicity cone and cone-semigroup construction are exact matches once their homogeneity and positivity assumptions are restored.

Access failures remain recorded. For example, a publisher metadata page is not promoted to full-text verification when its PDF timed out. The evidence files give source URLs, theorem/page locators and the actual scope of the match. No failure to retrieve an article is treated as evidence that the mathematics was never developed.

## 7. Proofs, computations and the second check

The development is deliberately confined to one precise part of one class-C question. Over the complex numbers, it fixes the differential Ore field with `[p,x]=1` and its rational subfield `C(x)`. A formal pseudodifferential-series embedding proves the centralizer of `x` is `C(x)`. Restriction to the rational function field then gives the fractional-linear stabilizer normal form. Conjugation fixes `x` only when the implementing element lies in that centralizer, so a rational translation is inner exactly when its shift is a negative logarithmic derivative of a rational function. Partial fractions turn this into the zero-polynomial-part, simple-pole, integral-residue criterion. The translation image in the outer automorphism group follows by quotienting an explicitly described additive subgroup. Finite-group splitting is a direct average of the translation cocycle.

These proofs state their hypotheses and do not infer a global classification. They distinguish torsion in the outer group from finite order of an actual automorphism, and distinguish preserving a chosen rational subfield from preserving the polynomial Weyl algebra. The finite-group proposition assumes that the group already preserves that rational subfield. No assertion that every finite group can be brought into that stabilizer is supplied.

Two exact runs of `code/check_weyl.py`, with seeds 1965 and 20261109, each passed 60 commutator tests, 60 inverse tests, 40 composition tests, 300 independent monomial-action tests, 2 finite-group checks, 175 PBW counts and 3 logarithmic-derivative implementation checks. Two deliberately incorrect formulas failed, as required: the opposite conjugation sign and an omitted chain-rule factor. The written independent review is in `evidence/development_review.md`. The computation output is retained; it is evidence against algebraic or coding errors, not a proof by sampling of the infinite statements.

Additional critical annotations were checked independently. The torus function sin x + sin y − sin(x+y) has exactly the three stated critical points; the two nonzero points have Hessian determinant 9/4, and the origin has cubic leading term xy(x+y)/2. A cutoff Hamiltonian realizes a small graph of its differential. The top cubic was re-derived by eliminating cyclic velocities from the actual recorded Lagrangian. The boundary-cosine identity was checked through the Jacobian from ordered endpoint pairs to unoriented lines and independently on a circle. The ellipsoid slice ratio depends on ab/c², while Fourier slice and Plancherel give the angularly averaged coefficient 8π². These calculations verify or refute precise readings; the angular average is not attributed to a notebook that does not write it. The supporting scripts and mathematical notes are supplied.

Two further independent reviews cover the early and middle explanatory arguments (`evidence/early_proof_review.md` and `evidence/middle_proof_review.md`). They confirmed the principal proofs and caught a false necessity claim in the weighted fixed-point count: unit local weights suffice, but unequal weights can also sum to the number of points. The corrected entry gives a checked real-projective-line example with weights 1/2 and 3/2. The final assembly also repeats the algebraically closed field hypothesis for classical coordinate rings, uses one consistent column-frame convention for the Chern connection, makes the smooth-division example a nonzero germ, and restricts the conditional viscosity estimate to the regular solution's interval of existence with an explicit matrix norm. These changes affect precision, not the classification counts.

The provenance marks `\ours` and `\oursComputed` mean “proved/computed here,” not “new to mathematics.” Known statements are cited and may also receive a complete proof here. The same distinction applies to elementary counterexamples in the critical annotations.

## 8. What the classes miss, and proposed method changes

The A–F scheme records a relation between an occurrence and a publication history, but cannot by itself describe correctness, fidelity, novelty or evidential strength. Four distinctions are indispensable this year. First, a later printed version can reproduce an earlier circulating exposition. Second, a speaker's correct intended theorem can be recorded in a literally false equation. Third, a later theorem can settle a reconstructed restricted question while leaving the notebook's omitted quantifiers genuinely unknown. Fourth, a useful bounded development may simply expose and prove a known normal form rather than produce a new theorem.

The assessment fields already address much of this. For later chapters, add (i) a correction-type enum, (ii) first-circulation and publication dates separately, (iii) source-heading/page-boundary confidence, (iv) an item-level speaker-of-intervention field, and (v) a theorem locator tied to each reference rather than one aggregated verification field. Keep occurrence IDs stable; revising a class should not renumber the archive. Continue to prohibit D on the basis of an unsuccessful first title search.

The supplied builder loaded the problem data but did not render the problem index. The delivered compatible copy adds that index, internal links, reciprocal-ID and sequential-item checks, and a readable standalone A4 driver. Corrections are rendered as wide, stacked entries because their mathematical formulas cannot fit legibly in five narrow columns. Long inline formulas receive display breaks without changing their content. Item titles wrap in a dedicated column beside a consistently aligned class badge, and each item has its own hyperlink destination. The complete DejaVu Serif family is bundled with its license for reproducible Cyrillic, italics and bold type; the style retains system-font fallbacks. A Babel locale fallback handles installations without the Russian language-definition package. Untouched supplied versions are retained under `code/kit_original/`. The seven data files keep the requested schema; the extra `area` field is solely an auditable counting aid. The format text calls them “eight files” but enumerates seven; the separate research report supplies the additional requested file.

The bounded search does not establish exhaustive bibliographic absence. The remaining Weyl question is dated and qualified accordingly. Some established statements have metadata or indirect verification only, and retain lower confidence or partial matching where appropriate. The chapter does not assert that all historical discussion has been recovered, that an indistinct phrase has a unique repair, or that the absence of class D proves there were no unrecorded original ideas.
