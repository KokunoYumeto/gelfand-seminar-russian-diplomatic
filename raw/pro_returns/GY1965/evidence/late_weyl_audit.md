# Bounded independent check: Aut_C Q(A_1), 2025–2026

**Checked 9 November 2026.** I found **no primary paper giving a full classification or generating presentation of Aut_C Q(A_1)** in the bounded recent-literature search described below. This is a search outcome, not a proof that the problem remains open. None of the positive recent hits supplies the missing full classification. I did not alter the root files or the late-record item counts.

## Primary distinctions checked against older sources

The following sources prevent several plausible but invalid positive identifications.

**Subfield stabilizers and finite invariants.** Dumas's author-hosted *Noncommutative invariants* (2011), [full text](https://lmbp.uca.fr/~fdumas/fichiers/B-Mat60.pdf), was read at §5.3.2, printed pp.63–64, and the concluding remark of §5.3.3, printed p.66 (PDF pages 67–70). The finite-invariant theorem first concerns groups in Aut A_1. The remark expressly separates general division-ring actions and describes the full Aut D_1(C) structure as unknown at that date. It gives the stabilizer of C(q), the stabilizer of C(pq), and an invariant-field result for finite groups preserving one of C(p), C(q), or C(pq). These restricted statements do not establish an unrestricted classification. The 1997 Alev–Dumas publisher URL was attempted but returned 403; the author-hosted account supplied the checked statements.

**Calogero–Moser stabilizers.** Berest, Eshmatov, and Eshmatov, *Dixmier groups and Borel subgroups*, [original preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.7356), [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.7356), introduction pp.1–2, defines G_n as automorphism groups of certain rings of differential operators Morita equivalent to A_1, identified with isotropy subgroups for the Calogero–Moser action. G_0 is Aut A_1. Those rings have a relation to the Weyl quotient field, but their automorphism groups are not asserted to exhaust Aut Q(A_1). The earlier [2011 primary announcement](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/era.2011.18.12) makes the same scope explicit.

## Assessment for the class-C entry

This check did not falsify the root's proposed C assessment by finding a recent full solution. It did strengthen the safeguards around that assessment:

- cite the precise established restricted results, not “automorphisms of Weyl algebras” generically;
- describe the unresolved remainder as the full division-ring automorphism group, with the exact generation/classification question the notes support;
- date the verification and say that a targeted 2025–2026 search found no full solution;
- do not infer present openness solely from this absence, from a Poisson analogue, or from a claim about polynomial algebra endomorphisms.

Dumas supplies an explicit historical statement of the missing full structure; the recent check supplies a bounded update rather than an omniscient negative assertion. If the root's modern C wording is stronger than that evidentiary scope, it should be softened accordingly.
