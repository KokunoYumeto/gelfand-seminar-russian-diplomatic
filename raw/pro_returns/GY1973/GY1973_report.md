## 3. The most consequential identifications

**Manin and the period measures.** The 1973 paper on periods of parabolic forms and p-adic Hecke series supplies exact locators for the integration criterion, the Hecke distribution relation, the corrected quadratic-root measure, modular-symbol reduction and ordinary interpolation. Earlier seminar occurrences in 1972-299 establish continuity with GP-1972-403. A paper's receipt date is not treated as a public circulation date. The discrepancy between the English printed receipt line and Math-Net metadata is recorded rather than silently resolved.

**Quillen's categorical constructions.** The full 1973 paper verifies theorems A and B, exact-category hypotheses, admissible spans and the identification of the first homotopy group of BQ with the Grothendieck group. Its introduction explicitly defers the proof of comparison with the plus construction. The chapter therefore cites the announcement with that limitation and a checked later account for the established comparison. Replacing BQ by its loop space and selecting the component is mathematically substantive. A finite-rank automorphism functor into Q is already nullhomotopic; it is not the required comparison map.

**Mishchenko's chronology.** The full 1974 paper was received inDecember 1972, but the stronger public evidence is the March–April 1973 two-page announcement. Its definitions and signature formula precede the October lectures. This changes the appropriate historical classification to E for the corresponding items, while retaining the 1974 paper as the fuller exact source.

**Stable rank and the zero-K2 dichotomy.** A 1974 Vaserstein–Suslin announcement repeats a false implication also present in the notebook. The later full paper explicitly separates additional zero-K2 fields from algebraic extensions of finite fields. The chapter keeps the false field classification, the genuine stable-rank theorem and the later resolution as different items. An exact publication match cannot turn a false premise into a correct theorem.

**Finite-gap theory and dressing.** Novikov's 1974 paper and Zakharov–Shabat's 1974 paper give close formula-level matches. The finite-gap density question is settled; the checked Grébert–Kappeler–Pöschel theorem gives the real Sobolev formulation and credits Marchenko–Ostrovskii's 1975 proof. Vague universal Hamiltonian claims and an incomplete collision-amplitude estimate are not promoted to open problems. Shabat's 2014 interpolation paper is unusually valuable because it explicitly names the 1974 Gelfand-seminar discussion, beyond merely treating the same broad subject.

**The final spectral-gap theorem.** A title search followed to Shnirelman's 1975 two-page note recovered the precise statement for the square-root Laplacian on a smooth near-Euclidean two-torus. The minimum of the two neighbouring gaps, the quantifiers and the spectral normalization all matter. The later theorem is by a named author other than the catalogued speaker, so it is class B. A parenthetical theorem attribution is not treated as proof that the author spoke at the meeting. The separate unquantified “twin” problem remains unidentified.

**Bibliographic and version checks.** The original Jónsson scan prints295–314, resolving a conflicting current publisher metadata range. The complete Atiyah bibliography distinguishes the 1973 Atiyah–Bott–Patodi heat-equation paper from the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer spectral-asymmetry announcement without changing the notebook's ink. For arithmetic coherence, a checked revision of Brody–Fisher–Mj–van Limbeek replaces an overbroad earlier author-hosted preprint. The retained positive statement remains conditional.

## 5. Independent checks

The Bernoulli moments were derived independently from the finite power-sum identity, using standard Bernoulli numbers with B1=−1/2. Exact rational calculations check 120 combinations of prime, moment and subdivision level (p=3,5,7; k=0,…,9; m=1,…,4). The limiting formula comes from an algebraic identity and p-adic polynomial continuity, not from extrapolating those 120 cases.

The conic construction was rederived algebraically and then checked by independent exact arithmetic over the Gaussian rationals. A 6-by-6 rational grid verifies the Bézout polynomial identity within its explicit degree bound; additional genuinely complex substitutions provide a separate parameter check. A coefficient-arithmetic check supplied with the conic appendix is independent of this grid computation. The reason the finite grid certifies that polynomial identity is the degree bound, not a numerical heuristic.

The tag-dependence counterexample and the categorical naturality arguments were rederived independently. The spring verification script records exact checks of the Lax, KP, soliton and related emendations in its stated scope. The proofs, scripts and outputs must be read together: a symbolic identity checks the equation written there, not all analytic existence or spectral hypotheses of a broader theorem.

Schema checks enforce consecutive item identifiers, the 1973 academic-year prefix, valid assessment values, citation-list completeness, reciprocal problem links and bibliography verification flags. ## 6. What the A–F classes miss

The main difficulties are orthogonal to historical fate:

1. **An assertion can be published and false.** The zero-K2 field dichotomy is an example. Its publication match and its correctness must remain independently searchable.
2. **A known theorem can have a false notebook formula.** The FPU nonlinear sign, p-adic logarithm sign, moment normalization, Floquet normalization and categorical degree shifts require mathematical emendations rather than guessed transcription repairs.
3. **A talk can precede the printed paper and follow a public announcement.** The public announcement places a result in E; a private receipt date alone does not establish that status.
4. **An exact modern answer may require an explicitly chosen topology or category.** The finite-gap density formulation and stable-rank questions illustrate why the reconstruction field is indispensable.
5. **F is often a statement about the source.** A cancelled expression, a missing quantifier or an undecidable oral attribution does not establish that an idea was never developed.

The proposed method change is modest: preserve the historical classes, but add structured fields for statement hypotheses, source versus critical date, actual source span, first public circulation, manuscript receipt, publication version, exact theorem locator and the scope actually read. A distinct development value such as “critical verification” would describe this chapter better than overloading “annotated” or implying a new research result.

## 7. Problem identity and earlier years

GP-1972-403 is reused with its earlier occurrences. The r-subspace classification entry links 1970-198 and 1972-277, but is not merged with GP-1970-23, the different deformation/rigidity question. The plus/Q comparison is thematically related to the earlier higher-K-theory comparisons; the earlier supplied title alone does not authorize conflating their different constructions. Similarly, group coherence is not sheaf coherence, finite presentation over a finite-field Laurent ring is not a question over the integers, and real unimodular reduction is not the complex question.

Only definite questions enter the problem index. The uncertain agenda surname, unspecified fundamental-group class, broad Hamiltonian conjecture and damaged amplitude/gap remarks stay in the occurrence index with their evidential limitations.

## 8. Presentation on the companion website

A reader arriving at one talk should first see its short summary, critical date and source-page span, followed by the mathematical items in source order. The scan or diplomatic passage should be one click from each item. The modern statement should display its hypotheses alongside the source wording; the reconstruction field should remain visible rather than being hidden inside the fate paragraph.

The next useful paths are the definite-problem page, the exact publication locator and the dated present status. Cross-year occurrence links should identify whether they mean the same quantified problem or a thematic antecedent. Records 331/332 need both catalogue and corrected dates. Overlapping catalogue ranges should not send a reader to the preceding talk's theorem.

Search and filters should expose correctness, verification scope, publication match and historical class separately. A class A filter alone would otherwise return a published false dichotomy without warning; a class F filter alone could be mistaken for a list of neglected research opportunities. A source-order field, structured scan location, optional intervention attribution, theorem-level evidence link and version-specific bibliographic identifier would support the necessary navigation. The data remains in the supplied fixed schema; the extra statistics and area map are auxiliary files.

## 9. Limits of the edition

The current-status statements mean that the dated searches and checked primary sources support the stated status. They are not a proof that no unpublished or unindexed result exists. Where only metadata or an abstract was available, no detailed theorem is attributed to that unread source. A failed direct retrieval is recorded, and an alternative successful primary reading is identified when available.

The chapter supplies modern hypotheses to make mathematical statements meaningful, but labels them as reconstruction. It does not invent missing diagrams, unstated estimates, unspecified boundary conditions or a research problem from an agenda label. The selected elementary checks do not verify every proof in the literature; item-level verification describes the narrower evidential claim.

## 11. Reproduction and files

The seven required data/TeX/bibliography files are at the archive root, together with this report, the generated chapter and compiled test PDF. The supplied builder, style and format specification are included. The additional area map and statistics file make the report counts reproducible. The code directory contains the mathematical checks, assembly and validation scripts; the logs directory contains their outputs and the TeX/BibTeX logs. Research and source-audit notes are under audit.

With a complete XeLaTeX installation, the prescribed build is:

    python build_chapter.py GY 1973
    xelatex GY1973_test
    bibtex GY1973_test
    xelatex GY1973_test
    xelatex GY1973_test

Alternatively:

    python code/build_pdf.py
    python code/validate_data.py
    python code/independent_checks.py

The conic coefficient check is code/fragments/autumn_representations/check_conic.py, with its recorded output beside it. The spring checks are code/fragments/spring_integrable/code/verify_emendations.py and require SymPy 1.14.0; their JSON and text outputs are retained in the adjacent output directory. These checks can be run from the archive root. Original fragment generators are included as research history and may require the original source inputs; they need not be rerun to build the chapter from its authoritative final data.

The helper uses the same supplied builder and XeLaTeX/BibTeX sequence. In this runtime, TeX packages were installed but their generated filename database and format were absent; the helper searched the installed distribution directly and generated a format in the writable workspace. No mathematical text, style or builder was weakened to bypass a compilation error. Four DejaVu Serif faces and their license are bundled so that English, Russian and italic text render consistently.

The manifest records the final file hashes. Source inputs and publisher PDFs are not duplicated into the deliverable; their identity and the checked publication links are documented in the audit and bibliography.
