## Orbit geometry and multiplicities

The exact questions were separated from proposals for student research. A negative eigenvalue does not define a hyperbolic generalized Cartan matrix; no matrix, module or weight is supplied, so that programme remains F. The SU(3) diagram is a regular six-dimensional orbit with a two-torus moment map. A regular level is four-dimensional, while its reduction is two-dimensional. The notebook really writes the level set in the Riemann–Roch formula and omits the quotient; this is a faithful mathematical error, not a transcription correction.

The Guillemin–Sternberg publisher record was opened at <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01398934>. It establishes October 1982 publication. The notebook is evidence of preprint circulation by March, but the full original theorem was not read. The July 1997 preprint of Meinrenken and Sjamaar, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/dg-ga/9707023>, was read in the introduction and §§2.1–2.4 for the precise reduced-space Riemann–Roch index and singular extension; its journal publication is 1999. No general singular quantization theorem was projected back into the 1982 notebook.

Atiyah's original convexity paper was opened at <https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~azinger/mat562/Atiyah82.pdf>. Its received date, 27 March 1981, is distinct from publication in 1982. Earlier item 1980-508.15 supplies seminar evidence of circulation. The Schur–Horn coadjoint example is older than the general torus theorem. Berezin–Gelfand's Math-Net record and full Russian text were opened at <https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mmo51>; the original records receipt on 28 June 1955 and a talk on 6 December 1955. The 1956 paper is only a partial match to the historical attribution, not evidence that it contains the later multiplicity theorem.

Kirillov's <https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/faa2766> and its full Russian original were read at §3, Theorem 2, equations (26)–(28), pp. 52–53. The dimension/volume identity uses the orbit through lambda plus rho with the specified normalization. Rescaling an invariant measure to have a desired total mass is a different, tautological statement. DLMF <https://dlmf.nist.gov/15.6>, equation 15.6.1, was opened to check the fractional-integral computation: its regularized hypergeometric convention must be converted to the ordinary function. The missing factor 1/Gamma(-p) is visible in the scan and confirmed by direct beta integration. The multivariable programme was compared with GKZ at <https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/faa1015>; that 1989 result is a thematic successor, not an exact solution of a formulated notebook conjecture.

## Givental and the exact Goldsheid metric

The English Arnold–Givental chapter at <https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/arnogive.pdf> was read at Chapter 2, §1.3, pp. 24–25. It gives the relative Darboux theorem and the local two-form extension criterion. Russian original metadata were verified at <https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/intf33> (1985); Springer metadata <https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-662-06793-2> establish the 1990 first English edition. Equality of pullbacks to the submanifold is sufficient locally after the fibrewise linear alignment; constant rank of the pulled-back form is not an extra hypothesis silently added here. The extension criterion is closedness and corank no greater than codimension in an even-dimensional ambient manifold.

Givental's 1983 announcement was opened at <https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/rm3010>, with both English pages read through the site's full-text route. Examples 1–2 and the theorem/corollary match the oriented-line and odd-binary-form constructions; receipt was 19 July 1982. The globally correct line space is T* S², with T* R² only a chart. The odd-form alternating coefficients were independently differentiated under translation. The Hamiltonian conserves q1; fixing p1 is a quotient slice, not another conserved quantity.

The original P001018 explicitly specifies the **norm of the difference of orthogonal projectors**, with proposed constant 1/sqrt(2), followed by the weaker request for some constant less than one independently of dimension. Searches included Goldsheid, Goldsheyd and Гольдшейд with Lagrangian subspaces, projections and coordinate planes; equivalent symmetric-unitary/diagonal-unitary norm approximation; MathOverflow and arXiv variants; the Math-Net author record <https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/person21867> and the author's institutional page <https://webspace.maths.qmul.ac.uk/i.goldsheid/>. Results included substantial surname noise (Goldschmidt and Goldstein) and general Maslov-chart statements. No exact later answer to this metric/quantifier combination was found. This is explicitly a negative retrieval result, not proof of global openness.

The full Mehrmann–Poloni published paper was read at <https://fph.altervista.org/publications/files2/MehP12a_permutedLagrangian.pdf>, especially Theorems 3.1 and 3.4 and their proofs. The actual off-diagonal entry bound is **sqrt(2)**; a search extraction suggesting its reciprocal was corrected from the original. Entrywise graph bounds are not dimension-independent operator-gap bounds.

The complete development uses S=UUᵀ and P=(I+SC)/2. It was independently rederived by a separate research agent, including all conjugations, projector conventions, constants and endpoint qualifications; see `goldsheid_audit.md`. The explicit three-by-three Fourier block produces a real Lagrangian six-plane and refutes 1/sqrt(2). Odd Fourier blocks give the universal lower obstruction cos(pi/8). A largest-empty-eigenvalue-arc argument gives cos(pi/(2n)) in each fixed dimension; it does not settle the uniform question. The coordinatewise vector observation is included only to show why separate vector-distance estimates do not control the span's projection gap.

`explore_goldsheid.py` first sampled random planes and performed numerical phase searches. Fifteen feasible values all lay below 1/sqrt(2), including 0.6794957284 for one n=6 sample. They were never treated as a universal proof; the structured Fourier counterexample demonstrates the danger. `verify_root.py` then checked the exact projector formulas with two independent seeds and several dimensions, the n=2 sharp example, the coordinatewise counterexample to a proof strategy, Fourier sizes m=3,5,7,11 and entrywise-square norms. Two numerical m=3 searches gave feasible gaps near 0.866026, with `success=False`; these are upper bounds from admissible points, not certified minima. The rigorous lower bound there is approximately 0.854313. The mathematical theorems do not depend on optimizer convergence. Complete scripts and outputs are supplied.

## APS, eta regularity and spectral cuts

The full APS I paper <https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps001.pdf> was read in the introduction and at Theorems (3.10) and (4.14). Receipt on 25 March 1974 differs from 1975 publication; the 1973 announcement was separately verified through the author bibliography <https://celebratio.org/atiyah_mf/article/36/>. The exact normal-symbol bundle identification, self-adjoint tangential hypothesis, nonnegative projection and zero-mode term were checked. The signature formula's even-form eta convention differs from the general index formula with (eta+h)/2. The notebook itself acknowledges that APS used self-adjointness; its normal-operator extension remains only partially identified. Neither vague boundary-domain choices nor unspecified real-part spectral cuts are silently endorsed.

Gilkey's full 1981 original <https://pages.uoregon.edu/gilkey/dirPDF/gilkeyAdvances.pdf>, Theorem 0.1 and introduction, establishes global eta regularity at zero in all dimensions without a filling, while allowing nonzero local residue. His first-edition book <https://pages.uoregon.edu/gilkey/dirPDF/InvarianceTheory1Ed.pdf>, §§1.7 and 1.10, was consulted for heat/zeta conventions and kernel subtraction. The all-positive div–grad–curl matrix in the scans has the wrong square under conventional differential operators. The exact polynomial-symbol calculation in `verify_root.py` checks both that matrix and the corrected self-adjoint one. The notebook's earlier R⁴ reference is also distinguished from the R³ base of standard curl.

Karras–Kreck–Neumann–Ossa's full original <https://ncatlab.org/nlab/files/Karras-Kreck-Neumann-Ossa_CutNPaste.pdf>, Chapter 1 and Corollary (1.4), p. 7, was read for the closed-manifold SK theorem. Its axioms cannot simply be equated with Gelfand's unspecified boundary additivity. The broad claim that signature is the only additive invariant also needs qualifications about Euler characteristic and gluing operations.

Wodzicki's publisher records <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01404760> and <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01403095> establish February 1982 and February 1984 publication. The first precedes the April seminar. Neither original full text was accessible, so exact equation-level priority between the two articles is not asserted. Ponge's full version 8 (25 January 2006), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0310102>, was read at Theorems 4.2–4.3 and Remarks 4.4–4.5. First preprint circulation was October 2003; journal identity was corroborated at <https://raphaelponge.org/research/>. The cut-difference residue formula and cut independence at zero are exact checked matches. Theorem 6.3 was not used because later corrections are known.

An additional audit prevented a false extension: regularity of cut zeta functions at negative integers does not force equality of their values for different cuts, even for differential operators. Loya–Moroianu, <https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.2478/v10309-012-0040-5.pdf>, §4, pp. 67–68, was read for negative eta/absolute-zeta poles of Dirac-type operators in odd dimensions. Combined with the exact cut-difference identity, these defeat the notebook's unrestricted nonpositive-integer extension. The related primary preprint <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.4039v2> was also checked for its distinction between cut-zeta regularity and absolute/eta poles and its Appendix B corrections. A tentative argument using shifted zero values was rejected because lower negative-pole contributions cannot be discarded. The final chapter contains neither that argument nor its false conclusion.

The shift polynomial in equation (20) was rederived from the finite Pochhammer coefficients, with fixed admissible cut, invertibility and a small scalar shift. Its residue term requires t^(k+mu), not t^k alone. Forty-two exact symbolic checks, k=0,…,6 and mu=1,…,6, corroborate the coefficient. This is a correction/verification annotation of an A thread, not a manufactured C research problem. The Mahler comparison was checked against the original scan <https://carmamaths.org/resources/mahler/docs/139.pdf>; no p-adic interpolation theorem for the unspecified spectral data was inferred.

The Stredder and Epstein DOI and Project Euclid routes failed repeatedly. Their titles and page ranges were corroborated in the opened full Fegan–Steer paper, cited in the bibliography, but both original entries retain `note = {unverified}`. Similarly, Seeley's Math-Net translation record <https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mat458> was opened without checking the original sign convention deeply enough to endorse the notebook's allegation of a published sign error. The surname Fuks is actually visible in the characteristic-class remark; it was not replaced by Losik from a familiar related title.

The final independent audit supplied an explicit source-independent counterexample: the rank-four massive flat Dirac operator on the 2pi-periodic three-torus has square (-Delta+mu²)I and absolute-zeta residue -2pi mu⁴ at s=-1. Assigning arguments +pi and -pi to its negative eigenvalues yields regular cut-zeta values -i pi² mu⁴ and +i pi² mu⁴. The full proof, all 16 Clifford anticommutator checks, the characteristic polynomial, heat-volume normalization, Mellin residue and both branch limits are included. Root independently rederived the residue and phase signs before acceptance.

## Interpretation limits

F items are not assertions of unsolved mathematics. They include unformulated hyperbolic multiplicities, group/representation duality, the multivariable hypergeometric programme, unrestricted boundary-additivity language, unspecified characteristic classes and a spectral/p-adic continuation. E means the qualified identifiable result predates the talk, even if its notebook wording is false. A may be a partial match to a speaker's later treatment, and the first 1982 Wodzicki article already predates the seminar. Verification annotations distinguish an opened record from an actually read theorem. No private recollection or inferred intention is offered as evidence.
