## Main findings and chronology

1. **Harish-Chandra regularity is exposition, including the Lorentz-wave example.** The Lie-algebra theorem was opened in full, and Galina–Laurent's precise finite-ideal formulation was read. For the notebook's Lorentz form, the adjoint identification with `sl_2(R)` and the one-generator invariant differential algebra make the nonzero-polynomial question an old special case. It is not a new PDE regularity problem. The full 1965 group paper could not be retrieved; its metadata were opened. Consequently the group-paper lemma numbering and the exact printed descent formulas have metadata-level or partial verification, explicitly stated in the items. The differential, PBW and transpose reconstructions do not pretend to be quotations from that paper.

2. **Gelfand's cited 1947 date is not silently turned into a publication date.** The matching paper is *Center of an infinitesimal group ring*, 1950, received 3 July 1948. The scan clearly reads 1947. The trace-invariant construction occurs in Section 2, example 1, pp. 105–106; the associated enveloping-algebra generation mechanism is Section 3, Theorem 2, p. 108. Earlier circulation was not established.

3. **Dynin's global pseudodifferential/Hodge/index exposition predates the 1967 talks.** The English 1966 survey was opened; it was received in March 1965. Exact local definitions and product/normalization statements were located. The isolated claim that homotopy invariance and additivity alone give a unique index is false: zero and integral multiples give counterexamples. The exact later six-axiom presentation was not found in the survey and therefore receives only a partial publication match.

4. **Earle–Eells is explicitly a report of other authors.** The original three-page Bulletin article was read in a reproduction after direct AMS access failed. It was communicated to the journal on 6 February 1967 and printed in July. Communication is not evidence of public circulation on that date; the items make this distinction. Class E reflects the notebook's explicit reporting of an already obtained Earle–Eells result, rather than an assertion of priority for Arnold. The later print date alone does not turn the report into Arnold's new theorem. The Lie-group remark refers to Leslie via the contemporary article, but Leslie's full paper was not read.

5. **The March 27 global boundary question has a strong exact contextual successor.** Initial searches in Dynin, Simonenko and broad boundary-operator literature did not justify treating arbitrary global completion as solved. The decisive primary match was Vishik and Eskin, *Normally solvable problems for elliptic systems of equations in convolutions*, received 27 April 1967 and published that year. Its introduction explicitly removes the earlier constant partial-index restriction. It has the same normal family, the same `G,B,E` augmented system, jumping kernel/cokernel dimensions, and in Section 3, Theorems 1.3 and 2.3, the criterion that the family index be a signed trivial-bundle class. These details support B for the precise reconstructed question. The manuscript's overwritten connective between `G` and `E` remains unreadable. The earlier 1967 survey, received December 1965, was read separately and does impose constant integer partial indices in its global theorem; the two papers are not conflated. The class assumes trivial auxiliary bundles and admissible Sobolev orders: allowing arbitrary bundles changes the obstruction.

6. **S. Gelfand's report is exactly Bourbaki 319.** Atiyah's lecture was February 1967, before the March 27 report, although the volume is dated 1968. Sections 1–2 match the notebook definitions, cycles, criteria and residue formula. The June 1967 addendum is explicitly later, and only appears in present-status discussion. Petrovsky's original 1945 paper was opened; its first pages already identify related 1943/1944 publications, which were not separately verified. The full catalogue pagination is 289–370, including the final Russian summary; Atiyah's reference ends at 368. Atiyah–Bott–Gårding (1970) was also opened for later context and its distinction between general lacunas and the notebook's zero-solution or strong lacunas. That distinction prevents an apparent contradiction between later terminology and the older theorem.
